Topic #347425: Some more lessons
  Author: CPMP
  Posted: 2022-08-24 04:11:19.596000
  Votes: 107  Comments: 19

The main reason I spend so much time on Kaggle is learning.  There is no other place where one can learn state of the art practical machine learning that effectively. Entering competitions, then contrasting our own solution with that shared by top teams is invaluable. This competition is no exception, and I am already learning while reading top teams writeups. Congrats to them for both the result, and the knowledge sharing! 

Reading top solutions confirms that token classification models was the way to go.  Yet I tried token classification only the day before last. The fact that I totally missed the right approach made me think. 

I compared what I did right (a 2nd solo prize in the NBME competition) and what I did not so right (this competition).  Here is my list so far. 



Team. Teaming is a net positive. Always. It certainly is not a reason for a worse result here. My team mate is not to blame for that result in particular.

Enter Early. Kaggle competitor skills increase every day, and now it is very difficult to make a difference in few weeks. In NBME I entered only few weeks after the start of the comp, and I had time to experiment lots of things. 

Check assumptions. I assumed that essays were too long to be fed in a LM transformer and had to be decomposed somehow. Given that I had a very scalable NLP model from AI4Code I reused it. I only tried to tokenize essays as whole the day before competition end, only to see that Deberta was happy to ingest text up to 2048 tokens.  I spent the whole competition on the wrong model because of a wrong assumption!

Start simple. In NBME I started with a simple token classification model and gradually added bells and whistles in both the model and in the training pipeline. Here I started from my complex AI4Code pipeline and tweaked it. It was wrong on two points: not a simple baseline, and not the right model at all.

Read. For NBME I read past NLP comp top solutions and their code. I also read a lot of NLP papers about adversarial training.  Here I assumed I knew enough. That's so wrong.

Learn. For NBME I experimented all the methods I read in papers. I learned a lot. Here I learned nothing. 

Don't put all your eggs in the same basket In NBME I really tried a lot of diverse methods. Here I refined a single model till last week of the competition. Refining a model is fine once you know it is better than every other reasonable method you can think of.  

Check by yourself I read reports that using previous competition data either by MLM or pseudo labeling did not work here. I only tried PL once, and only saw a minor improvement. I trusted what I read instead of testing thoroughly. As a result our selected submissions do not leverage the previous competition data at all. How silly is that?

Assume you are a rookie.  Kaggle is so competitive that each competition resets everything. What was a key innovation in a competition becomes baseline knowledge in the next one. That you got a prize in a similar competition few months ago does not mean you know better than any other competitor now. I clearly was too confident in my NLP knowledge here.


That's my 2 cents. There is probably more and I may update this post if there is interest.
