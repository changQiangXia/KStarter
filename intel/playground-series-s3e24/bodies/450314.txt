Best of luck for this episode! I hope the below domain information helps one and all to engineer better features for the challenge-

Domain elements

1. BMI



It is the ratio of weight (kg) / height (metre-squared)

BMI &lt; 18.5 - Underwieght

BMI between 18.5- 24.99 - Normal

BMI between 25- 29.99 - Overweight

BMI between 30-34.99 - Obese- grade 1

BMI between 35- 39.99 - Obese- grade 2

BMI &gt;= 40.00 - Obese- grade 3 


One could peruse more information in the below link- 



https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolic_rate

https://www.healthline.com/health/what-is-basal-metabolic-rate


2. Waist circumference and obesity

For men-



below 94cm (37in) - low risk

94–102cm (37-40in) - high risk

more than 102cm (40in) - very high 


For women- 



below 80cm (31.5in) - low risk

80–88cm (31.5-34.6in) - high risk 

more than 88cm (34.6in) - very high


More information is available at-



https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/medical/measuring-your-waist#:~:text=Why%20does%20your%20waist%20measurement,excess%20fat%20inside%20your%20organs.

https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/obesity-prevention-source/obesity-definition/abdominal-obesity/


3. Age and health risk



Age &lt; 45 - lower risk

Age &gt;= 45 years - high risk
This is as per ACSM norms 


3. Blood pressure classification



Systolic BP &lt; 120 mmHg or Diastolic BP &lt; 80 mmHg - normal

Systolic BP between 120 mmHg- 129 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 80-89 mmHg- elevated 

Systolic BP between 130 mmHg - 139 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 90-99 mmHg- stage 1 high BP

Systolic BP between 140 mmHg - 179 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 100-119 mmHg- stage 2 high BP

Systolic BP &gt;= 180 mmHg or Diastolic BP &gt;= 120 mmHg- stage 3 high BP (emergency)


More information can be found in the below links-



https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure

https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/classification-of-blood-pressure-in-adults


3. HDL- LDL cholesterol classification

Total cholesterol-



Less than 200mg/dL    Desirable

200-239 mg/dL    Borderline high

240mg/dL and above    High


HDL-



Less then 40- High risk

Between 40-60 - Normal

Greater of equals 60- Low risk


LDL-



Less than 100mg/dL- Optimal (best for your health)

100-129mg/dL- Near optimal

130-159 mg/dL- Borderline high

160-189 mg/dL- High

190 mg/dL and above- Very High


More information can be found in the below links-



https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cholesterol-levels/

ASCM guidelines


4. Triglyceride classification



Less than 150 - normal

150- 199- moderate risk

200-499- high risk

500+ - very high risk


More information can be found in the below links-
1.https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459368/#:~:text=According%20to%20National%20Cholesterol%20Education,TG%20%3E%20500%20mg%2FdL.



ASCM guidelines


5. Haemoglobin classification for anemic cases



Children aged under 5 years and pregnant women  &lt;110 g/L - high risk for anemic case

Others- &lt;120 g/L - high risk for anemic case


More information can be found in the below links-



https://www.who.int/data/nutrition/nlis/info/anaemia

https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9448346/


6. Creatinine normal range



For adult men - 0.74 to 1.35 mg/dL (65.4 to 119.3 micromoles/L)

For adult women - 0.59 to 1.04 mg/dL (52.2 to 91.9 micromoles/L)


More information is found as below-



https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/creatinine-test/about/pac-20384646

https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/322380


7. γ-GTP normal levels-



normal range for adults is 5 to 40 U/L

values above 40 are considered risky for liver disease


More information is found as below-



https://www.ucsfhealth.org/medical-tests/gamma-glutamyl-transferase-(ggt)-blood-test#:~:text=The%20normal%20range%20for%20adults,or%20may%20test%20different%20specimens.

https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22055-gamma-glutamyl-transferase-ggt-test


8. AST- ALT levels-



The normal range of an SGOT test is generally between 8 and 45 units per liter of serum

Higher results indicate risk for liver disease


More information is found as below-



https://www.healthline.com/health/sgot-test

https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/320982

The below links can furnish relevant information-
