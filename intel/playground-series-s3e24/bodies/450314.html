Domain elements

1. BMI

  1. It is the ratio of weight (kg) / height (metre-squared)
  2. BMI < 18.5 - Underwieght
  3. BMI between 18.5- 24.99 - Normal
  4. BMI between 25- 29.99 - Overweight
  5. BMI between 30-34.99 - Obese- grade 1
  6. BMI between 35- 39.99 - Obese- grade 2
  7. BMI >= 40.00 - Obese- grade 3

One could peruse more information in the below link-

  1. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolic_rate
  2. https://www.healthline.com/health/what-is-basal-metabolic-rate

2. Waist circumference and obesity

For men-

  1. below 94cm (37in) - low risk
  2. 94–102cm (37-40in) - high risk
  3. more than 102cm (40in) - very high

For women-

  1. below 80cm (31.5in) - low risk
  2. 80–88cm (31.5-34.6in) - high risk
  3. more than 88cm (34.6in) - very high

More information is available at-

  1. https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/heart-matters-magazine/medical/measuring-your-waist#:~:text=Why%20does%20your%20waist%20measurement,excess%20fat%20inside%20your%20organs.
  2. https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/obesity-prevention-source/obesity-definition/abdominal-obesity/

3. Age and health risk

  1. Age < 45 - lower risk
  2. Age >= 45 years - high risk
    This is as per ACSM norms

3. Blood pressure classification

  1. Systolic BP < 120 mmHg or Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg - normal
  2. Systolic BP between 120 mmHg- 129 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 80-89 mmHg- elevated
  3. Systolic BP between 130 mmHg - 139 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 90-99 mmHg- stage 1 high BP
  4. Systolic BP between 140 mmHg - 179 mmHg or Diastolic BP between 100-119 mmHg- stage 2 high BP
  5. Systolic BP >= 180 mmHg or Diastolic BP >= 120 mmHg- stage 3 high BP (emergency)

More information can be found in the below links-

  1. https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure
  2. https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/table/classification-of-blood-pressure-in-adults

3. HDL- LDL cholesterol classification

Total cholesterol-
  1. Less than 200mg/dL Desirable
  2. 200-239 mg/dL Borderline high
  3. 240mg/dL and above High
HDL-
  1. Less then 40- High risk
  2. Between 40-60 - Normal
  3. Greater of equals 60- Low risk
LDL-
  1. Less than 100mg/dL- Optimal (best for your health)
  2. 100-129mg/dL- Near optimal
  3. 130-159 mg/dL- Borderline high
  4. 160-189 mg/dL- High
  5. 190 mg/dL and above- Very High

More information can be found in the below links-

  1. https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cholesterol-levels/
  2. ASCM guidelines

4. Triglyceride classification

  1. Less than 150 - normal
  2. 150- 199- moderate risk
  3. 200-499- high risk
  4. 500+ - very high risk

More information can be found in the below links-

5. Haemoglobin classification for anemic cases

  1. Children aged under 5 years and pregnant women <110 g/L - high risk for anemic case
  2. Others- <120 g/L - high risk for anemic case

More information can be found in the below links-

  1. https://www.who.int/data/nutrition/nlis/info/anaemia
  2. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9448346/

6. Creatinine normal range

  1. For adult men - 0.74 to 1.35 mg/dL (65.4 to 119.3 micromoles/L)
  2. For adult women - 0.59 to 1.04 mg/dL (52.2 to 91.9 micromoles/L)

More information is found as below-

  1. https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/creatinine-test/about/pac-20384646
  2. https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/322380

7. γ-GTP normal levels-

  1. normal range for adults is 5 to 40 U/L
  2. values above 40 are considered risky for liver disease

More information is found as below-

  1. https://www.ucsfhealth.org/medical-tests/gamma-glutamyl-transferase-(ggt)-blood-test#:~:text=The%20normal%20range%20for%20adults,or%20may%20test%20different%20specimens.
  2. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22055-gamma-glutamyl-transferase-ggt-test

8. AST- ALT levels-

  1. The normal range of an SGOT test is generally between 8 and 45 units per liter of serum
  2. Higher results indicate risk for liver disease

More information is found as below-

  1. https://www.healthline.com/health/sgot-test
