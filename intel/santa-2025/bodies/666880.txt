I ran a Rust and C++ implementation on my local machine with 24 CPUs for 10 days (N&lt;60). My results indicate that asymmetric solutions are actually superior to the symmetric patterns found in public notebooks (01-09-2026 11:42am EST).

Results for N &lt; 60:



















The difference is clear: asymmetry yields better scores. The bottleneck for N &gt; 60 is strictly computational power.  Since I am working on a laptop,  I use Sparrow (Rust) for initialization and a custom C++ SA solver for deep search. By utilizing Intel TBB (), Atomics, and a custom geometry kernel (no bloat), I achieved ~10.56 million iterations per second.

The Challenge: If anyone has a better symmetric solution for these specific numbers (N 16 35 36 40 51 52 54 57 59), I would be genuinely interested to see it. However, my data currently points strictly towards asymmetry.

Update: I have just started running this strategy for N &gt;= 60. Sadly, this is going to take a very long time…
