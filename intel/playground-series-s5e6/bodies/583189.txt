Topic #583189: N/P/K Ratios: The Hidden Signal in a Low-Corr Dataset
  Author: Younus_Mohamed
  Posted: 2025-06-05 10:13:44.152000
  Votes: 68  Comments: 39

After a week of poking around the 750 k row fertilizer dataset I’m finally happy with the shape of my exploratory analysis and wanted to share 
(1) the strongest signals I’ve spotted, 
(2) the modelling choices I’m about to make, and 
(3) the feature-engineering plan that links the two.



1.  What the EDA is shouting at us

Class &amp; categorical structure



Target balance is good – the seven fertilizers all live in the 12–16 % band. No need for resampling tricks.  

Strong joint patterns in (Soil Type, Crop Type). The heat-map of fertilizer share by (Soil, Crop) clearly shows preferred pairings (e.g. Clayey–Millets → 28-28).  

Zero missing values – we can focus on meaning rather than cleaning.


Numeric behaviour



Every numeric column is quasi-uniform within its physical bounds (histograms are almost flat).  

Almost no correlation between numerics (|ρ| &lt; 0.01 in Spearman map).  

Nevertheless, the box-plots per target reveal that each fertilizer shifts the median of Nitrogen, Phosphorous, Potassium, etc. by a few units.


Visual hunches that matter



Stacked histograms by fertiliser show nutrient density bumps at different ranges – that screams for ratio / interaction features rather than relying on raw values.  

Scatter-matrix and 3-D plot confirm that the joint distribution of (Temperature, Humidity, Moisture) carries class signal even if pairwise correlations are low.




2.  Model line-up (and why I’m betting on them)



LightGBM / CatBoost - Usually fast, loves categorical splits, baseline “floor” in &lt; 5 min. 

XGBoost (hist) - Different boosting implementation → decorrelated errors for ensembling. 

TabNet or shallow MLP-Mixer - Captures non-linear nutrient ratios and climate interactions that trees sometimes flatten.

kNN in nutrient sub-space - Exploits local patterns after scaling; cheap diversity model. 




3.  Feature engineering roadmap




Nutrient chemistry  



Ratios: N/P, N/K, P/K, plus total N+P+K.  

Quartile bins – one-hot encode “Low/Med/High” nutrient levels.  


Soil-Crop interactions  



Target (mean) encoding of the 55 (Soil, Crop) combos.  

A hashed combo feature Soil_Crop_ID for models that don’t ingest categoricals.


Climate aggregates  



Temp-Humidity Index = Temperature × Humidity / 100.  

Rolling z-scores within each fertilizer to capture relative micro-climate.  


Keep it lean – no dimensionality reduction yet; numerics are only seven columns.




4.  Training &amp; blending plan



Phase 1: Tune LightGBM with Optuna (~200 trials) – early-stop at 200 rounds.  

Phase 2: Replicate optuna search for XGB (hist) – fix learning rate first, search depth/leaves second.  

Phase 3: Quick TabNet run with default scheduler; retain only if CV gain &gt; 0.003.  

Phase 4: Build kNN (k ∈ [5, 11, 21]) on StandardScaler-ed nutrient-only frame.  

Phase 5: Weighted soft-vote; weights learned via Optuna on out-of-fold preds.  


Calibration (CalibratedClassifierCV, sigmoid) will be checked last; if log-loss drops without hurting MAP@3 or MAP@5, I’ll submit the calibrated ensemble.



5.  What I still don’t know (and will test next)



Does adding a global soil-quality index (Moisture / Temperature) explain any residual confusion?  

Will pairwise GMM clustering of nutrient ratios reveal hidden groups?  

Can a simple rules-based model outperform k-NN on extreme nutrient cases?




TL;DR



The data are clean, balanced, low-corr, but class medians differ → engineer nutrient ratios.  

Tree ensembles + one NN + a distance model gives a diversified stack.  

Target-encoded (Soil, Crop) is likely the single most valuable categorical trick.  


I’ll update once the baseline ensemble is on the board.  
All feedback and any suggestions are welcome.

Lets fertilize! Happy Kaggling.!
