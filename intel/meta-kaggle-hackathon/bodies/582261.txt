Back in 2018, I launched a study group for high school students interested in data science. It started with one school, grew to four by 2023, and has now evolved into an open YouTube channel. Many of my students have competed on Kaggle, placing in the top 20-30% entirely through their own code. This year I have some a student take 3rd place in Playground competitions. It is a free, online class open for everyone. You might have noticed us on Kaggle leaderboards ("KNAI" prefix in the team name). 

A photo from the start of the least year edition:



I guess this is not related to Meta Kaggle dataset, and thus not eligible for this competition, but I thought I would share it anyway, as it relates to the broad impact Kaggle has on ML / DS community.

I truly believe Kaggle has the potential to transform how we teach data science because of:



Competitions: They create an engaging, game-like environment perfect for younger audiences.

Notebooks: The shared environment ensures accessibility and consistency, with free compute for training models—an invaluable resource for learners.


Let me tell you a story on notebooks. Before we coded via kaggle notebooks, everyone would bring their own laptop. One student would bring Windows, another Linux, another would bring Mac. Just to install Python with relevant packages on everyone's laptop took us 3-4 classes, and some students did not manage it at all. Switching to notebooks was an incredible experience - they just register and everyone can code from the start! And think about code sharing and compatibility! Kaggle notebooks are an amazing product for education in ML, especially at early stage.

Why do I do it at all?

Working in ML in online Advertising, at some point I was struggling with questions of meaning of my professional work.
