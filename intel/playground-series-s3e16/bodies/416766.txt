Topic #416766: #1 Solution - Experimenting with the data generation model
  Author: epikt
  Posted: 2023-06-13 01:16:13.891000
  Votes: 24  Comments: 21

This was a fun competition. To make a long story short, the selected entry that got first place was a "safe" ensemble that simply averaged previous models made using different approaches (described below). The goal was to have something that would anchor me somewhere in the top 10 in case of a big shakeup, and it seemed to succeed. My second submission was a more experimental - at least for me - ensemble where I used Optuna to try to find the best weight to give to the predictions of a bunch of models, using the out-of-fold predictions and Optuna to find the best weight coefficients for each. This worked somewhat, but was too rushed and the final MAE was a bit higher (1.33566). The main reason I ended up selecting two ensembles as final submissions is that I found some of the data to be a bit random, so I thought it was the safest approach.

To go into more detail, the submissions of the first two days were just baselines, both with regular models and automl ones (mainly MLJar, which had really good results, and Autogluon).

The next few submissions were experiments with models trained on additional data. I generated a lot of data and trained on over two million rows, but it didn't improve the results much. Since that didn't work, I tried to understand the data generation model better and found that a development version of the library used for this competition had a function to impute missing data. I thought the competition was over, and posted about it, but after trying it a bit more, the results were too random at high temperatures, and didn't match the targets enough at low ones. Since that didn't work, I switched to trying the sampling function of the same library. Basically, it converts the dataframe rows into randomly permuted sentences ending with "Age is" to then get the target as the next word prediction. I let this run for maybe two days, but the data generation model still didn't give me anything close enough to the real targets.

I then moved on to loading the data generation model into the HuggingFace Transformers library to explore it a bit more. I tried taking the logits of the different target tokens, averaging them, and then training on them Pytorch Tabular, Tab Transformer and Catboost on them. The results were ok, but not great, so I moved on to fine-tuning the data generation model as a sequence classification model - still using the Transformers library, with the targets as the different classes - and in retrospect, that particular submission would have been my best if I had trusted it a bit more (1.33383).

So mostly it was a lot of experimenting, trying to trust my CV a bit, the public LB a bit, and averaging things in the end to play it safe and prevent too big a drop. Catboost pretty consistently gave me the best results. My whole process was a bit messy, and most of my notebooks are all over the place; at one point, I was training on over 130 features. Maybe other solutions will be more interesting or detailed, but if anyone has any questions, I'd still be happy to try to answer them, if not, thank you to the organizers, I learned a lot in this competition and the last few ones I participated in, this was fun!
