tattaka &amp; mipypf part

Summary



2.5D and 3D backbone and decoder without upsampling


1/32 Resolution is sufficient for this task

By focusing resources on encoder, more layers can be used for training

Strong regularization


Data preprocessing



Group K-fold is used for the validation, but is divided into 3 parts because of the large ink_id=2

Input resolution is 256x256 for 2.5D model and 192x192 for 3D model


Cut out and store in npy in 32x32 (64x64 when inferring) to speed up data loading

The label is downsampled to 1/32 resolution using bilinear interpolation

Input images were normalized for each sample　


  
  mean = img.mean(dim=(, , ), keepdim=)
  std = img.std(dim=(, , ), keepdim=) + 
  img = (img - mean) / std


Model



images(batch_size x channel x group x height x width) -&gt; 2dcnn backbone -&gt; pointwise conv2d neck -&gt; 3dcnn(ResBlockCSN like, 3 or 6 blocks) -&gt; avg + max pooling(z axis) -&gt; pointwise conv2d


2dcnn backbone: 


resnetrs50

convnext_tiny

swinv2_tiny

resnext50

The channel/group combinations used were 5x7 and 3x9. In other words, the middle 35 or 27 layers of the 65 layers are used.

Referring to @tereka's model architecture

images(batch_size x 1 x layers x height x width) -&gt; 3dcnn backbone -&gt; max pooling(z axis) -&gt; pointwise conv2d


3DCNN backbone: 


resnet50-irCSN(layers: 32)

resnet152-irCSN(layers: 24)

loss: bce + global fbeta loss(calculate fbeta score in batch)


Training



amp

EMA(decay=0.99)

label smoothing(smooth = 0.1)

drop_path_rate=0.2

cutmix + mixup + manifold mixup

heavy augmentation


cutout


modified to match the output resolution (1/32)

channel shuffle


Shuffle within the group after splitting

Random +-2 shift in z-direction of volume with 0.5 probability  

other transforms


albu.Compose(
          [
              albu.Flip(=0.5),
              albu.RandomRotate90(=0.9),
              albu.ShiftScaleRotate(
                  =0.0625,

              albu.OneOf(

                  ],
                  =0.9,
              ),
              albu.RandomBrightnessContrast(
                  =0.3, =0.3, =0.3
              ),
              ToTensorV2(),
          ]
      )


Inference



fp16 inference

stride=32

Use weights learned on val_ink_id=(1, 2a)

TTA


h/v flip

Switch tta each time stride 

@philippsinger proposed percentile threshold


Always predicts the same amount of positives, so it is independent of model performance and depends on the pos/neg ratio of the GT

Calculated for all pixels except the area outside the mask

0.9 and 0.93 were used

We expected 0.90 to be a better score in private, but 0.93 was better in the end!


yukke42 part

Summary



3D encoder and 2D/1D encoder


1/2 or 1/4 resolution prediction

very simple decoder
​


Data preprocessing



split the 2nd fragment into two image vertically: 4 folds
​


Model



3D CNN encoder and 2D Encoder


encoderbased on @samfc10 's Notebook: Vesuvius Challenge - 3D ResNet Training


remove maxpooling after the 1st CNN

use attention before reduce D-dim

use resnet18 and resnet34

decoder


a single 2D CNN layer

upsample with a nearest interpolation

output resolution is downsampled to 1/2. then upsample with a bilinear interpolation

3D transformer encoder and linear decoder


encoder: use MViTv2-s of the PyTorch official implementation and a pre-trained model


modify forward function to get each scale output

replace MaxPool3d into MaxPool2d for the reproducibility

decoder: a single linear and patch expanding to upscale low resolutions 3D images


patch expanding is from Swin-Unet

output resolution is downsampled to 1/2 or 1/4, then upsample with a bilinear interpolation
​


Training



amp

torch.compile

label smoothing

cutout

cutmix

mixup

data augmentation


referred @tanakar 's notebook: 2.5d segmentaion baseline [training]

referred @tattaka and @mipypf 's

patch_size=224 and stride=112


stride=75 or stride=56 didn't work
​


Inference



fp16 inference

stride=75


better than stride=112

ignore edge of output prediction


use only the red area of prediction (figure below)
