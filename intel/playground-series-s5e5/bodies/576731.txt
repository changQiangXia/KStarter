Predict Podcast Listening Time (Playground Series S5E4)

Competition Link  
Regression task to predict how many minutes listeners spend on each podcast episode.




@cdeotte (opens in a new tab)"&gt;1st Place – RAPIDS cuML Three‑Level Stack by @cdeotte  
• Built 73 diverse level‑1 models (LightGBM, XGB, RF, SVR, KNN, linear, MLP) on GPU with RAPIDS; target encoding and heavy feature synthesis supplied ~6 000 columns.  
• Added auxiliary models trained without Episode_Length_minutes to handle its 12 % missing rate, then let a non‑linear level‑2 XGB decide which sub‑model to trust row‑wise.  
• Two rounds of chronological pseudo‑labelling and five‑seed bagging for every layer reduced drift when predicting unseen weeks.  
• Careful fold design (10 × GroupKFold on episode IDs) kept CV within 0.01 RMSE of both public and private boards.  
• GPU throughput (A100) enabled 11 h total training despite &gt;500 M predictions generated for stacking features.


@greysky (opens in a new tab)"&gt;2nd Place – Single LightGBM with Target Encoding by @greysky  
• Leveraged extensive target and pair encodings; categorical pairs/triples were picked by grid‑search on 30‑fold CV.  
• Drop‑in polynomial terms and rank‑based binning captured long‑tail effects without inflating cardinality.  
• Public notebook stressed the value of median aggregation (instead of mean) for noisy TE targets.  
• Final model used 8 000 leaves, monotone constraints on length‑related columns, and early‑stopping at 4 700 trees.  
• Achieved top‑tier accuracy with &lt;30 min single‑GPU runtime, demonstrating the ceiling of a well‑tuned “solo” GBDT.


@johannesheller (opens in a new tab)"&gt;3rd Place – Forward Feature Selection &amp; Stacking by @johannesheller  
• Started from 1 300 engineered columns and iteratively kept only additions that improved 10‑fold RMSE ≥ 0.002.  
• Core stack: CatBoost, LGBM goss, ElasticNet, shallow TabNet; Ridge was used during greedy search to preview ensemble lift.  
• Final two‑level hill‑climber re‑weighted 42 retained OOF columns for an extra −0.015 RMSE.  
• Careful seed‑averaging (seven seeds per GBDT) smoothed variance introduced by aggressive max_depth settings (&gt;18).  
• Notebook details a reusable “feature vault” design that stores every generated column once and joins on demand.


@ravi20076 (opens in a new tab)"&gt;4th Place – Lots of Features, Simple Models, Ridge Blend by @ravi20076  
• Trained 382 compact models (LGBM gbdt/goss, XGB, CatBoost, AutoGluon) on brute‑forced bigram–7‑gram column interactions.  
• GPU A6000 + 256 GB RAM processed 925‑feature matrices in &lt;15 min per model; deeper trees gradually ceded to shallower ones as feature count rose.  
• Ridge stack on z‑scored predictions held perfect CV/LB correlation; CV 11.614 → public 11.645 → private 11.542.  
• Post‑processing rounded every prediction to the nearest true target value, squeezing an extra −0.0005 RMSE.  
• Discussion quantifies cloud‑GPU spend (~150 USD) and shows why diversity from weaker CatBoost runs still mattered.


@optimistix (opens in a new tab)"&gt;5th Place – 100 OOFs, Laziness, and a Few Blunders by @optimistix  
• Collected &gt;100 out‑of‑fold predictions from LGBM and XGB variants with differing fold counts (10 vs 30) and encoding schemes.  
• Relied heavily on @greysky’s TE code and @masayakawamata’s selected‑feature notebooks to save GPU quota.  
• Demonstrated that clipping forecast tails (|pred| &gt; 99.9 pct) avoided disastrous private‑LB jumps (un‑clipped model scored 177 RMSE!).  
• Ridge beat Lasso and Autogluon stackers when all 100 OOFs were fed, but pruning to 70 models improved robustness.  
• Post‑mortem lists “lazy” ideas never tried (OpenFE, TabNet, residual modelling) and how each could have nudged the score further.


@masaishi (opens in a new tab)"&gt;6th Place – RMSE‑Driven Feature‑Combo Search by @masaishi  
• Verified three dataset leaks: high‑precision Episode_Length, abnormal Number_of_Ads, and duplicate‑row groups; overwriting them cut 0.02 RMSE.  
• Generated &gt;200 handcrafted features: cyclical sin/cos for day/time, integer‑fraction splits, sentiment dummies, polynomial degrees up to 3.  
• Exhaustive RMSE scan over 1‑ to 4‑column combos picked four high‑coverage group‑mean replacements.  
• Ensemble of five learners (HistGBR, LGBM, XGB, TabNet, SVR) blended via ridge; added TabNet residual fit for rows with &gt;2 missing values.  
• Full experiment lifecycle tracked in WandB; repo shows modular data‑class + model‑interface pattern enabling 1 000+ automated runs.




Forecast Sticker Sales (Playground Series S5E1)

Competition Link  
Daily sales forecasting for 2017‑19; evaluation is MAPE.




@georgekoussa (opens in a new tab)"&gt;1st Place – Extended Ratio Decomposition by @georgekoussa&nbsp;|&nbsp;Notebook  
• Adopted @kdmitrie’s GDP × store × product × DoW × sin‑cos framework then re‑fitted each ratio with SciPy MAPE‑optimised linear models.  
• Holiday impact added through 10‑day Gaussian windows around each country’s statutory holidays via holidays package.  
• Normalised GDP per‑capita with (-17 643 + 85.42 × GDP)/365 transform, giving Kenya a bespoke slope to fix under‑prediction.  
• Derived store, product and country factors by regressing de‑composed residuals on wave features up to 9th harmonic.  
• Final constant 1.06 multiplier justified by year‑over‑year error plot; rounding to int improved public MAPE but preserved private rank.


@cdeotte (opens in a new tab)"&gt;2nd Place – Transformer on Linear‑Residuals by @cdeotte&nbsp;|&nbsp;Public Starter  
• Base multiplicative linear model captured main ratios; 3‑layer transformer with positional encoding learned residual seasonality.  
• Autoregressive inference 35 steps ahead; two pseudo‑label rounds (train → +2017/18 → +2019) curtailed error propagation.  
• Five‑seed median ensemble plus 30‑holiday boolean features lifted public MAPE from 0.0526 to 0.0486.  
• Explored future‑trend uncertainty: submitted both constant‑1.06 and mild‑linear‑up multipliers, the former winning privately.  
• Discussion details why stacking (Transformer learning residuals) beats simple averaging and how CV gap shrank to 0.001.


@kdmitrie (opens in a new tab)"&gt;3rd Place – Joint Parameter Fit via SciPy by @kdmitrie&nbsp;|&nbsp;Notebook  
• Represented sales as one multiplicative formula with eight factors: GDP, store, product, weekday, sin‑cos, country, country‑day, trend.  
• All coefficients solved simultaneously with scipy.optimize.minimize under MAPE loss, avoiding sequential bias.  
• Gaussian‑shift holiday response (+4.5 days, σ² = 8) convolved across 10‑day windows; holiday‑observed vs actual treated separately.  
• FFT illustrated pitfalls of naive denoising; regression on Fourier basis chosen to avoid boundary artifacts.  
• Found that dropping 2010‑12 Kenya rows helped public LB but hurt private; final two submissions differed only by this choice.


@stopwhispering (opens in a new tab)"&gt;4th Place – Sequential Ratio Model with FFT Smoothing by @stopwhispering  
• Derived year‑totals from ensemble average, then sequentially estimated country→store→product→DoY ratios.  
• FFT smoothed product‑share curves separately for even/odd years to capture two‑year periodicity.  
• Compared flat vs linear‑trend extrapolation for 2017‑19; constant variant proved superior privately.  
• Discussion explains why discrepancy in GDP‑based country ratios required post‑hoc linear regression correction, especially for Kenya.  
• Implementation in pure Pandas runs &lt;10 min CPU, emphasising interpretability over heavy modelling.


@cabaxiom (opens in a new tab)"&gt;5th Place – Trend‑vs‑Constant Probe Ensemble by @cabaxiom  
• Same decomposition as @kdmitrie but produced two prediction sets: one following 2016‑trend slope, one flat.  
• Submitted both; constant path scored far better privately, illustrating sensitivity of MAPE to future‑year scaling.  
• Holiday deltas estimated from median of previous seasonally‑adjusted years, including three Kenyan holidays missing from library.  
• Employed groupby‑median smoothing to tame noise before multiplying holiday factors, improving public LB by 0.002.  
• Notebook lists further unexplored artefacts (e.g., leap‑day handling) that could explain residual 0.0008 gap to podium.


@pascalterpstra (opens in a new tab)"&gt;6th Place – Triple‑Model Blend by @pascalterpstra  
• Model‑1: @kdmitrie multiplicative ratios tweaked with 1.02 Kenya‑only and 1.06 global multipliers.  
• Model‑2: Separate regressions for daily fraction of annual total, plus store‑/product‑fraction regressors on sin/cos features.  
• Model‑3: Transformer from @cdeotte public starter; supplied as an additional OOF column for ridge.  
• Ridge blend of 81 OOFs chosen; later test with Autogluon stacker (missed deadline) would have improved to 0.0462.  
• Feature importance analysis shows OpenFE‑generated 140 columns shaved 0.005 MAPE off every GBDT baseline.




Backpack Weight Prediction (Playground Series S5E2)

Competition Link  
Estimate a hiker’s packed‑weight from synthetic gear characteristics.




@cdeotte (opens in a new tab)"&gt;1st Place – Single‑Model 500‑Feature XGBoost by @cdeotte  
• Generated 1 600+ candidate features with RAPIDS cuDF; retained 500 that consistently improved 20‑fold CV, yet a 138‑feature T4‑GPU variant was still leaderboard‑winning.  
• For every categorical (and numerical treated as categorical) built seven encodings; crafted 28 n‑gram combos, NaN base‑2 hash column, weight‑capacity digit splits and rounding bins.  
• Introduced novel groupby‑histogram counts and multi‑quantile aggregations; merged original student‑bag prices as MSRP proxy, each shaving 0.005 RMSE.  
• Trained 20 000‑tree GPU‑hist XGB (lr 0.001, depth 10) on A100 80 GB in six hours; only colsample_bynode reduced as feature pool grew.  
• Integer rounding post‑process trimmed leaderboard score by 0.0003 while keeping CV/private gap below 0.001.


@ravi20076 (opens in a new tab)"&gt;2nd Place – Century‑Scale Ensemble with Ridge Stack by @ravi20076  
• Engineered 1 600+ features across nine datasets (Polars + RAPIDS), including 28 categorical n‑grams and CuML target encodings (mean/median/count/nunique).  
• Trained 65 boosted‑tree bases (21 XGB, 20 LGBM, 21 CatBoost, 3 autoencoder‑enhanced) under 20‑fold CV; single‑model scores ranged 38.64–38.76 RMSE.  
• Added dense‑NN classifier and supervised autoencoder latent vectors for diversity, then built 35 BayesianRidge meta‑learners on Colab TPUs.  
• Final three‑layer stack blended 100 components through ridge, attaining 38.629 RMSE private; integer‑rounded variant served as safer backup.  
• Write‑up details GPU/TPU scheduling, feature store design and experiments that failed (sample weights, variance TE, leap‑row fixes).


@automatylicza (opens in a new tab)"&gt;3rd Place – Autoencoder Features&nbsp;+ Four‑Tree Stack by @automatylicza  
• Built supervised autoencoder whose 32‑dim latent vector (trained to reconstruct weight and predict price) fed downstream regressors.  
• Created distance‑based similarity features between categorical embeddings and combined every category with weight capacity.  
• CuDF group statistics, multi‑quantile bins and NaN hash indicators enriched tabular signal; external bag‑price dataset supplied orig_price_* stats.  
• Four base models (LGBM, XGB, XGB‑alt, CatBoost) trained with 10‑fold CV; BayesianRidge stacked OOF predictions.  
• Small public‑notebook blend nudged private RMSE to 38.64, securing the final podium spot.


@optimistix (opens in a new tab)"&gt;5th Place – NoiseStack Needle Hunt by @optimistix  
• Compiled 25‑model ensemble (CatBoost, LGBM, XGB, AutoGluon) after mining community scripts; Ridge stack chosen to keep LB–CV gap at ~0.2.  
• Leveraged @cdeotte’s engineered features and @souradippal’s generator code to craft extra capacity‑driven columns without exhausting GPU quota.  
• Autogluon stack hit CV 38.59 but showed 0.26 gap on LB, signalling overfit; fallback Ridge ensemble CV 38.64 yielded 38.84 public / 38.63 private.  
• Late weighted blend with @mikhailnaumov’s public notebook propelled rank from 18 → 5, illustrating high‑risk public‑board stacking tactics.  
• Reflection section discusses motivation cycles, resource constraints, and the need to cherry‑pick community ideas instead of blindly stacking.




Predict Insurance Premium Amount (Playground Series S4E12)

Competition Link  
Regression task with RMSLE metric; predict synthetic insurance premiums.




@cdeotte (opens in a new tab)"&gt;1st Place – Single 611‑Feature XGBoost by @cdeotte&nbsp;|&nbsp;Notebook  
• Searched 145 k column combinations (2‑ to 6‑way) with GPU cuDF, retaining 170 that consistently improved 20‑fold CV.  
• For every categorical (original + combos) created seven encodings: label, CE and five TEs (mean/median/min/max/nunique).  
• Treated five numerical columns as categoricals for TE, then re‑attached original numeric versions.  
• Full model: 20 000 XGB trees (lr 0.001, depth 10, subsample 0.8); gradient hist GPU algorithm finished in 6 h (A100).  
• Public/private gap &lt; 0.001 thanks to 10‑fold CV × 10‑fold TE and ridge‑like L2 regularisation.


@noodl35 (opens in a new tab)"&gt;2nd Place – 118 OOF AutoML Ensemble by @noodl35  
• Generated OOFs with AutoGluon, H2O, FLAML, LAMA plus custom Cat/LGBM/XGB; stacked with Lasso, Ridge and TabNet.  
• FLAML folds run in parallel notebooks to respect Kaggle‑GPU limits; yielded five strong ExtraTrees variants.  
• Added class‑probability CatBoost OOF predicting target‑quantile buckets; improved ensemble by ~0.002 RMSLE.  
• OpenFE features donated by community shaved another 0.0015; final set 170 engineered + backpacker core.  
• Hill‑climb pruned to 81 OOFs before Lasso; private CV 1.027 → private LB 1.0258, validating robust weighting.


@optimistix (opens in a new tab)"&gt;9th Place – “With a Little Help from my Kaggle Friends” by @optimistix  
• Adapted top public notebooks to save their OOFs; blended 100+ predictions with Ridge, Lasso and Autogluon.  
• Switched to Lasso for final submission after noticing past comps where Lasso beat Ridge privately despite worse public.  
• Added NAN‑count feature and health‑score binning per @cdeotte/@swagician posts; each cut CV by ~0.002.  
• Autogluon meta‑stack (missed deadline) would have improved to 1.025 RMSLE; post details laptop resource juggling.  
• Season retrospective lists eight top‑10 finishes and lessons on overfitting when public/private splits diverge.




Regression of Used Car Prices (Playground Series S4E9)

Competition Link  
Predict synthetic used‑car prices; evaluation metric is RMSE.




@martinapreusse (opens in a new tab)"&gt;1st Place – Stacked Neural Network with OOF Features by @martinapreusse  
• Began with a classic Ridge blend of 20‑fold GBDTs/SVRs, then switched last‑minute to a fork of Vladimir Demidov’s Keras tabular NN.  
• Fed the NN four powerful numerical inputs: SVR OOFs, two LGBM OOFs (one high‑bin variant), and CatBoost‑classifier outlier‑probability.  
• CatBoost classifier produced leak‑free 20‑fold OOFs using median target encoding on categoricals to flag &gt;Q3 + 1.5 IQR outliers.  
• Additional FastAI (AutoGluon) and original‑dataset rows were used only inside training folds, keeping OOF length unchanged.  
• NN ensemble CV 72 468 RMSE narrowly beat the Ridge stack (72 300) and scored best on private board, delivering solo gold.


@gerlandore (opens in a new tab)"&gt;2nd Place – AutoGluon Weighted Ensemble with Targeted FE by @gerlandore  
• Simplified transmission into two buckets, tagged luxury brands, extracted cylinders &amp; HP from engine string, and built mileage_per_year.  
• Created several feature crosses (brand×model, brand×mileage, interior×exterior colour, etc.); rare categories binned via quantile noise flag.  
• AutoGluon’s weighted‑ensemble (FASTAI + LightGBM + CatBoost) found optimal model mixture; Optuna‑tuned single CatBoost served as fallback.  
• Augmented training with original used‑car dataset; cross‑checked via 5‑fold CV to avoid public‑LB overfitting.  
• Last‑day CV 71 980 jumped from public rank 626→2 privately, illustrating benefit of restrained FE plus AutoML.


@optimistix (opens in a new tab)"&gt;3rd Place – “Gather, Ridge, Repeat” Ensemble by @optimistix  
• Assembled 77 OOF files from Team LightAutoML, @roberthatch, @martinapreusse and personal runs; maintained identical fold length by trimming extras.  
• Ridge regression consistently beat Autogluon, hill‑climb and Lasso ensemblers in CV and private LB; best submission CV 71 808 → private 62 958.  
• Managed public/private divergence by capping LB‑CV gap at 0.2 and avoiding aggressive weight‑search on public board.  
• Observed that GPU‑T4 vs CPU Autogluon stacks on same OOF pool could differ by ±0.03 RMSE; chose CPU variant that ranked potential #1 post‑hoc (62 892).  
• Write‑up emphasises disciplined OOF management, late‑stage resource juggling and lessons from six consecutive top‑10 finishes.


@tilii7 (opens in a new tab)"&gt;4th Place – 32‑Model Hill‑Climb Stack by @tilii7  
• Constructed two diverse 32‑model ensembles: Keras and xLearn factorisation machines, AutoGluon sub‑models, Lasso, CatBoost, RandomForest, ExtraTrees, XGB, LGBM.  
• Used proper hill‑climbing optimiser with positive/negative weights and verified gains via 5‑fold CV; no “public‑board guessing”.  
• Added original‑dataset rows only inside training folds; inclusion of AutoGluon‑generated NLP n‑grams gave marginal +20 RMSE benefit.  
• Declined flashy FE after noting no private‑LB lift; cautions that random weight guessing on public LB (“blending”) is unreliable.  
• Post lists reproducible scripts for extracting AutoGluon child‑model OOFs and stresses need for common fold splits when mixing sources.


@innixma (opens in a new tab)"&gt;5th Place – Day‑1 AutoGluon Zeroshot Stack by @innixma  
• Submission was literally the first model trained 8 h after launch: AutoGluon 2024 zeroshot portfolio with experimental tweaks.  
• Hacked numeric label into stratified K‑folds, preventing fold imbalance due to extreme outliers; dropped original features in L2 to rely solely on stack inputs.  
• Disabled text n‑grams/special features to cut runtime; used new use_orig_features=False flag for cleaner meta‑learning.  
• Despite mediocre public rank 626 (RMSE 72 221), strong 20‑fold CV predicted private success — ended at 5th (RMSE 62 977).  
• Post‑mortem attributes public/private mismatch to different outlier density and underscores value of trusting robust CV over leaderboard noise.




ASHRAE – Great Energy Predictor III

Competition Link  
Multisite time‑series regression to forecast hourly building‑meter energy (electricity, chilled‑water, steam, hot‑water) for &gt;1 000 buildings; metric is RMSLE.




1st Place – Team Isamu &amp; Matt (CatBoost / LGBM / MLP blend)  
• Aggressively cleaned training data: removed three anomaly types (long constant streaks, bidirectional spikes, visually spotted metre glitches) using site‑level cross‑checks, then linearly interpolated missing temperatures.  
• Corrected local‑weather time‑zone mismatch and converted meter_reading with log1p, plus extra models that standardised by square_feet for diversity.  
• Feature set remained compact yet rich: raw building &amp; weather vars, cyclic hour/day encodings, holiday flags, Savitzky–Golay smoothed temps ± derivatives, categorical interactions and Bayesian target encoding.  
• Trained separate CatBoost, LightGBM and MLP models per (meter, site) and per (building, meter), using rolling‑month validation variants to harvest diverse OOF predictions.  
• Final ensemble used Optuna‑tuned generalised weighted mean on log‑space predictions and averaged four top submissions (added three leak‑free public kernels) – yielding the gold‑medal 1.08 RMSLE on private board.


2nd Place – Team cHaOs (rohanrao et al.)  
• Manual visual sweep dropped low‑value noise and vertical zero streaks for every one of the 1 449 buildings – biggest single boost.  
• Deliberately limited engineered features (&lt; 30) to favour stability; instead trained dozens of models per site × meter, per building × meter and global – XGB, LGBM, CatBoost plus site‑wise FFNN (only for electricity).  
• Post‑processing: multiplied predictions by 0.80–0.85 (chosen via leak hold‑out &amp; CV) to correct inflated mean after noise‑row removal; lifted RMSLE by ≈ 0.01.  
• Ensemble weighting: ≈ 30 % XGB bag, 50 % LGBM bag, 15–20 % CatBoost, 5 % FFNN on meter 0; weights hand‑tuned with leak and public board feedback.  
• Validation combined 4–5‑fold month‑block CV with leak rows; robust to public/private split, finishing at 1.09 RMSLE and $7 000 prize.


3rd Place – chabir (Simple Averaging + LightGBM Meta‑stack)  
• Focused on advanced cleaning: script eliminated simultaneous zero readings across all meters within a site‑timestamp, dramatically reducing spurious “vertical lines”.  
• Generated a variety of models (CNN, LightGBM, CatBoost) on multiple cleaned/feature variants; feature ideas included lag temperatures, count encodings and a solar‑radiation proxy computed from latitude, DOY and hour.  
• As CV schemes proved unstable, retained &gt;30 diverse prediction files and averaged those with Pearson correlation filtering; simple mean already ranked sixth.  
• Built second‑stage LightGBM using these predictions (+ meter id) as features, trained solely on leaked rows, to down‑weight weak models – public LB dropped but private score jumped to bronze.  
• Lessons stressed that rigorous anomaly removal outweighed sophisticated feature engineering, and that leak‑informed stacking could rescue private‑board rank when public signal was noisy.




Santander Value Prediction Challenge

Competition Link  
Predict the monetary value of each customer transaction from fully anonymised numeric features; evaluated by RMSLE.




1st Place – lukeeee &amp; gilberto (Leak‑Driven Time‑Series Reconstruction)  
• Discovered 113 hidden 40‑column “feature sets” whose patterns repeat across rows; used them to align ~3 900 train and ~7 900 test rows into ordered client‑wise time‑series groups.  
• Implemented strict pair‑matching (lag ≥ 2) to label rows with leaked targets, then rebuilt complete sequences (lag 1) and hand‑corrected 9 mis‑aligned leaks, lifting private RMSLE by ≈ 0.016.  
• Generated many‑to‑many RNN inputs: for each sequence stored position, group size, leak flag and engineered size/leak ratios—fed to simple LSTM that predicted missing targets inside a group.  
• Brute‑forced additional feature‑set discovery: iteratively extended column groups ensuring exact equality on all series; accelerated with heuristics (minimum non‑zero, sequence length ≥ 5).  
• Noted dataset quirks: (i) six‑column mean relationships between feature sets, (ii) censorship of targets outside 30 k–40 M, (iii) public &amp; private boards scored only ~9 k shared test rows.


2nd Place – adilism (LightGBM/XGB/Random‑Forest on Sequence Aggregates)  
• Re‑created row/column orders via iterative matching but kept only long sequences (&gt; 5) at each step to minimise false positives; yielded cleaner client IDs for CV.  
• Custom 5‑fold CV ensured every client’s full sequence stayed within a single fold, reducing leakage between train/valid splits; averaged several random seeds to tame fold‑size variance.  
• Feature space: ~3 500 aggregations across all 96 detected sequences and their trailing 7/14/21/28‑day segments (non‑zero mean/max, exp‑smoothed means, counts, lags). Selected top ~ 1 000 by importance.  
• Added test leaks to training (data augmentation); small CV gain though effect on private LB unclear due to two‑decimal rounding.  
• Final ensemble blended LightGBM, XGBoost and RandomForest on the reduced feature set; weighted average out‑performed sophisticated architectures and secured silver ($15 k).


3rd Place – ilyenkov (Massive GBM Bag with Sequence‑Level Features)  
• Identified 110 reliable 40‑column groups (from possible 125) plus 97‑group subset; trained separate LightGBM and XGBoost models on each set as well as on sums of top groups for diversity.  
• Engineered statistics per group (log‑mean, log‑median, sums, std, kurtosis, non‑zero counts) and global row features; concatenated three most important groups into a “super‑group” and repeated stats.  
• Leveraged 7 9 5 4 test leaks (lags 35–37) as additional training rows; validated their correctness (≈ 99.8 %) before augmentation.  
• Applied GBM importance‑based feature selection, 4‑fold CV, then blended 23 models (majority LightGBM) with iterative weight tuning on combined OOF + leak predictions.  
• Observed “six‑property” mean relations among feature sets and hypothesised that only rows obeying these linear constraints were scored—a clue explaining public/private board behaviour.
