Yesterday I spent quite some time trying to get useful predictions with a random forest (and with ExtraTrees and with HuberRegressor).

I wasted my time: Today I realized that simple predictions without machine learning are much better than all my random forest attempts. It gives little consolation that a hundred other people wasted their time as well ☹️

The method is easily explained: We have to predict traffic congestion for one Monday afternoon. The train dataset contains the congestion values for two dozen Monday afternoons. If we compute the median congestion for every time and place and submit these medians, we get an lb score of 4.967 (notebook is here).

Why does this method work? Because the afternoon of September 30 is not much different from other Monday afternoons.

Of course this simple method neglects that not all Mondays are equal. Traffic congestion may be affected by weather, construction sites, events and so on. To take this into account, I'm going to try to use the morning traffic to predict the afternoon traffic. It is plausible that if morning traffic deviates from average, afternoon traffic will deviate from average, too.

My mistake was that I simply followed the format of the data: The dataset comes as a table with rows, and I tried to predict the congestion value of every row from the features of the same row. But this is not the character of time series. The specialty of time series is that every row contains a timestamp and that you have to use the data of several rows to predict the target of a future row. Using the morning rows to predict the afternoon rows is just an application of this principle.
