Hello all,

I have participated in quite a few tabular challenges lately and have experience of success and failure in these types of assignments across my tryst with these challenges. I wish to chime a few thoughts regarding this competition in line with my participation in past editions of the challenge (PIU and Sleep States) and similar sized dataset challenges too, hoping these points will help a beginner orient himself/ herself well.

Please feel free to agree/ disagree with these elements as they will surely help the reader orient him/ herself accordingly. 



The dataset appears huge with a 1.2 GB size but we have approx. 5100 sequences of data here in the training set. This translates to an effective data size of 5100 rows, making this a small data challenge

Past CMI competitions, especially the PIU challenge was known for its shakeups. Single models and very simple approaches prevailed while large and complex ensembles failed - this could be a directive for this competition too

The metric is prone to shakeup as a few well classified points will alter the score considerably while a few poor classifications could reduce the score considerably. Be careful to design simple models that generalize well rather than a lot of complex models with questionable generalization potential

This is a code competition with an API - beginners transitioning with the playground competitions may find the API a bit different. Please follow the standard submission kernel as a guideline and edit this for starters

Remember to test the prediction values on a dummy train set before submitting, especially if you are new to this type of competitions - erroneous submissions are counted in the daily quota, so be careful and submit correctly 

Non-CV backed blends and blind fuses rarely work in such assignments - stick to optimizing the CV scheme. You have 3 months going ahead and I am sure this competition does not require that long time to complete

Usually such competitions will attract a lot of participants and we could witness 4500+ teams at the end of the challenge as well. A good way to approach such competitions is to set one's goals well and try and realize them as one moves along. Usually setting easy goals and achieving them all along is better than setting a far sighted and difficult goal that could become difficult to realize.

Plan your time in August 2025 now and try and gauge if you could make time for this competition - this competition will conclude on Sep2, so your time in August is crucial

Start off with simple single models and make way for FE - this is crucial here. We don't need a baroque ensemble here- simple ML models seem to be sufficient if done well. Usually, leaders and toppers focus on FE in such competitions while the rest focus on model training - I am sure one's direction is clear here. 

Finally, keep track of your work with any experiment tracking tool. It could be a simple excel sheet, a tool like W&amp;B, Neptune.ai, MLOps tools like Kedro / MLFlow, etc. Experiment tracking is a good way to orient yourself with regard to ideas that worked and did not work and revisit your work later in the competition. Usually these artefacts are useful while teaming up as well as one could articulate the work done with aplomb.

Save the fitted models and OOF predictions as you move along and keep them handy for later use - this trick will surely help you at the end of the competition when you may want to blend and fuse submissions and models for just an extra bit of alpha


Finally, have fun - this is a fun-learning filled challenge aimed at making one better at ML models and data wrangling. One learns the best when one enjoys the work done! Try and learn along and improve yourself - the public forums will evolve with time and this is a crucial factor in your final submission. 

All the best for a successful competition and happy learning!
Best regards,
Ravi R
