BirdCLEF: Summary of Techniques from Past Top Solutions (2024)

This table summarizes key techniques and approaches observed in the write-ups of the top 10 solutions from a recent BirdCLEF competition (likely 2024, based on common themes like pseudo-labeling). It aims to provide insights and potential starting points for fellow Kagglers tackling BirdCLEF 2025.

Disclaimer: This is based on summaries and may not capture every nuance. Always refer to the original write-ups for full details.

Data - Source
Use only current year's data (train + unlabeled)
1st
Some used past years' data for pretraining or added specific samples.



Use external data (Xeno Canto)
3rd, 7th (for extraction), 8th (pretrain)
Mixed results; 1st, 4th, 7th found it didn't help or hurt. Quality/filtering is key.



Use unlabeled soundscapes (Pseudo-labeling)

Very common &amp; impactful. Key for domain adaptation. Often iterative.


Data - Preprocessing
Filter/clean data (e.g., using Google Bird Classifier, duplicates)
1st, 4th, 7th, 8th
Removing low-quality/irrelevant segments, correcting labels.



Use specific audio duration (e.g., 5s, 10s, 15s)
1st (10s), 2nd (5s), 3rd (5s), 4th (5s/15-20s), 5th (5s)
5s and 10s seem most common. Longer durations capture more context but increase compute.



Upsample/Downsample classes
3rd, 4th
Addressing class imbalance, e.g., ensuring minimum samples per class.



Cyclic Padding
1st
Repeating audio to meet fixed input length for short sounds.


Data - Augmentation
Mixup / Cutmix
1st (Cutmix), 2nd (Mixup), 4th (Mixup/Cutmix), 5th (Mixup), 8th (Mixup/Sumix), 9th (Cutmix/Mixup), 10th (Noise Mix)
Very common for regularization. Sumix (mixing based on signal power) also used.



Noise Addition (Gaussian, Pink, Background)
4th, 8th
Adding noise from datasets or synthesized noise.



Gain / Random Volume
4th, 8th
Randomly adjusting audio volume.



SpecAugment (Time/Frequency Masking)
1st (XY masking), 8th, 9th (worked against)
Masking blocks in the spectrogram. Mixed results reported.



Time/Frequency Stretching
2nd
Resizing spectrogram along time/frequency axes.


Input Features
Mel Spectrogram
1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 8th, 10th
Standard approach. Parameters (n_mels, hop_length, etc.) varied widely.



Raw Waveform Input
3rd (Aves), 4th, 5th
Using 1D CNNs or specialized models (like Aves) directly on audio samples.



PCEN
4th (didn't work)
Per-Channel Energy Normalization; an alternative to log-mel.


Model Architecture
EfficientNet (B0, B3, V2)
1st (B0), 2nd (B0), 3rd (V2), 5th (B0), 7th (V2), 8th (B0), 10th (V2)
Extremely popular. B0 often chosen for efficiency on CPU inference.



EfficientViT
3rd (b0, b1, m3)
Vision Transformer variant highlighted for speed and performance.



SED (Sound Event Detection) Models
3rd, 4th, 7th, 8th
Architectures often combining CNNs and RNNs/Attention for audio tasks.



Other CNNs (ResNet, SEResNeXt, NFNet, RegNetY)
1st, 4th, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th
Other common image classification backbones adapted for spectrograms.



Waveform Models (Aves)
3rd, 7th
Transformer-based models operating directly on raw audio.


Training - Loss
BCEWithLogitsLoss
3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th
Standard Binary Cross Entropy for multi-label classification.



CrossEntropyLoss (CE)
1st
Used by 1st place, notably combined with sigmoid in inference.



Focal Loss
4th, 8th, 9th (no improvement w/ CAD)
Addresses class imbalance by down-weighting easy examples.



Secondary Label Handling
1st (Weighted), 3rd (Masked), 8th (Primary)
Various strategies: ignore, down-weight, or treat like primary labels.


Training - Strategy
Pseudo-Labeling / Distillation
1st, 2nd, 3rd, 6th, 7th, 8th, 10th
Training on model's own predictions on unlabeled data. Often iterative.



Multi-stage Training (Pretrain -&gt; Finetune)
8th, 10th
Often pretraining on past data/larger datasets, then finetuning on target data.



Checkpoint Averaging / Model Soups
2nd
Averaging weights from multiple checkpoints or models for robustness.



Domain Adaptation Techniques
9th (CAD Bottleneck)
Explicitly training to reduce domain shift (e.g., using domain labels).



Split species into subsets
7th
Training separate models for subsets (e.g., rare vs. common species).


Inference/Postproc.
Ensembling (Mean, Min, Weighted, Geometric)
1st (Min), 2nd (Mean), 3rd, 4th (Weighted+Geo), 5th, 7th, 8th
Universal. Combining diverse models is key. Min-ensembling mentioned by 1st.



Temporal Smoothing (Moving Average, Convolution)
2nd, 3rd, 4th, 6th, 9th
Averaging predictions over adjacent time windows. Simple &amp; effective.



Inference Optimization (OpenVINO / ONNX)
1st, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 7th (Quantize), 8th
Crucial for meeting CPU time limits. INT8 quantization also used.



TTA (Test Time Augmentation)
4th (Time shift)
Less common than in vision. E.g., predicting on slightly shifted windows.



Probability Adjustments / Cut-offs
3rd, 4th
Post-hoc adjustments based on thresholds or soundscape-level predictions.




Key Takeaways



Domain Shift is Real: Bridging the gap between training data and noisy soundscapes (often via pseudo-labeling) was critical.

EfficientNets Rule (esp. B0): A strong and efficient default choice, especially considering inference constraints.

Ensembling is Non-Negotiable: Combining diverse models consistently improves results. Diversity can come from different backbones, input features, training data/folds, or hyperparameters.

Temporal Context Matters: Smoothing predictions over time is a simple but effective post-processing step.

Inference Speed is Key: Optimizing models (OpenVINO/ONNX, quantization) is necessary for the competition format.
