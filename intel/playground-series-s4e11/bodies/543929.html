What so we learn from this repeating pattern?

  • One may be tempted to move up the public leaderboard and focus on blending and fusing models based on the feedback from the public leaderboard. In both the competitions, I clearly remember that a lot of kernels focused on very questionable post-processing, mode-blending and copy-pasting public solutions with questionable sum-products and all such approaches failed on the private leaderboard while simple and even some single models prevailed.
  • Focus on your own process and always make a submission decision based on the CV score
  • I have been around on Kaggle for 3+ years now and have participated in all the playground episodes from Mar2022 till date and have seen my own rise with point 2 above and my own fall with any form deviation from point 2

I am sure that a lot of beginners will be reading the post here and wish to forebode the risks of any non-CV backed activities with such metrics that necessitate hard labels. The latent risks of questionable blends and ensemble solutions far exceeds their momentary benefits and is clearly discernable at the end of the month.

Why is blind blending so bad?

  • Consider 2 models, one with a Stratified-5-fold CV scheme and another Stratified-25-fold CV scheme. We have here, 5 model-mean predictions from model1 and a 25-model mean prediction from model 2
  • If we simply make a sum-product with 70%-30% allocation, we fuse models that have learnt different targets in their individual cross-validation schemes together, purely based on their collective response on a small test subset and submit. Is this desirable? The fusion in this blind gamble is not only not likely to succeed, but is a very incorrect way to structure models too that can never be used anywhere in/ outside of Kaggle.

How do we ameliorate this issue?

  • Stay away from kernels and discussions that encourage blends and ensemble solutions without a cv-backup.
  • Measure the cv-score across all your models and develop your own pipeline based on your + public ideas
  • Choose a final submission wisely and learn from past mistakes
