I'm very happy that in the 3 playground competitions I've done this year, I've come out well vs. the shake-up: +57 places last month, +312 the month before, and now +662 places this month. My final solution was a simple unweighted average of the predicted probabilites by

  • 6 LightGBMs combining target encoding, count encoding and the in-buildt encoding of LightGBM for categories, count encoding continuous values, and also having the continuous values as continuous features
  • 7 CatBoost models (trained with log-loss using 0 to 6 copies of the original data)
  • 6 CatBoost models (trained with focal loss as I had suggested in a discussion using 4 copies of the original data)
  • 1 fastai neural network with very few hidden units layers=[10, 5] (trained with focal loss, Mish activation and high weight decacy of wd=0.1 treating all features as categoricasl with the following embedding sizes: {'Name':3, 'Gender':1, 'Age':2, 'City':3, 'Working Professional or Student':1, 'Profession':3, 'Academic Pressure':2, 'Work Pressure':2, 'CGPA':2, 'Study Satisfaction':2, 'Job Satisfaction':2, 'Sleep Duration':2, 'Dietary Habits':2, 'Degree':3, 'Have you ever had suicidal thoughts ?':1, 'Work/Study Hours':2, 'Financial Stress':1, 'Family History of Mental Illness':1}. This was using various tricks I mentioned in this discussion - mostly importantly 40x seed averaging.
  • 3 Logistic Regressions that turned every variable into a one-hot-encoded category

Selecting a simpel average was a very deliberate decision. I had the impression that I was already overfittign my 10-fold simple CV and shouldn't even try to even weight on the out-of-folds (as I mentioned in the discussions). So, I ignored that some models like cb5 appeared much better than other models and what Hill climbing said (it pretty much wanted to just go with cb5, i.e. a CatBoost with 5x repats of the original data) and any suggestion that I should even weight the average of my models. As it turns out, I selected the very best of my submissions in terms of the private leaderboard by going for the most boring standard average.

Arguably, the other important thing may have been how I tuned GBDTs: I avoided overfitting the out of folds by training for too many iterations so that I could select the best iteration across all folds. I.e. not taking the performance and iteration per fold found by early stopping. This may have been especially important due to the overfitting potential of such a noisy metric as accuracy.
