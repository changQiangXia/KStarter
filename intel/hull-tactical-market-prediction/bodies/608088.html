If the answer to any of the following questions is yes, you're wasting your time:

  1. Have you submitted a notebook and got a leaderboard score above 3.0?
  2. Have you studied a public notebook with a leaderboard score above 3.0?

If you've answered yes at least once, there is compelling evidence that you are wasting your time:

  1. Sharpe ratios above 3.0 don't exist in real life.
  2. I've cross-validated the models of some public notebooks with high leaderboard scores, and they all got negative R2 scores. A dummy model which always predicts the average would get an R2 score of 0, and higher is better.
  3. The competition's data tab states: leaderboard scores … are not meaningful.
  4. You would never evaluate a model on its training data, would you? For the time being, the public leaderboard evaluates models on a subset of the training data; this leads to inflated scores and favors overfitted models.

You don't believe me yet? Go find these two lines in your notebook:

enet_model = (...)
enet_model(X_train, y_train)

or 

xgb_model = xgb.XGBRegressor
xgb_model.fit

Just insert the following lines and look at the output:

from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score

enet_model = ElasticNet(...)
enet_score = cross_val_score(enet_model, X_train, y_train).() # R2 
('CV R2 :', enet_score)
enet_model.(X_train, y_train)
(enet_model.coef_)

xgb_model = xgb.XGBRegressor(...)
xgb_score = cross_val_score(xgb_model, X_train, y_train).() # R2 
('CV R2 :', xgb_score)
xgb_model.(X_train, y_train)

If the R2 scores are negative or the ElasticNet coefficients are all zero, delete the notebook as quickly as possible!
