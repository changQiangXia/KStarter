  Author: AmbrosM
  Posted: 2025-09-17 17:43:45.435000

If the answer to any of the following questions is yes, you're wasting your time:



Have you submitted a notebook and got a leaderboard score above 3.0?

Have you studied a public notebook with a leaderboard score above 3.0?


If you've answered yes at least once, there is compelling evidence that you are wasting your time:



Sharpe ratios above 3.0 don't exist in real life.

I've cross-validated the models of some public notebooks with high leaderboard scores, and they all got negative R2 scores. A dummy model which always predicts the average would get an R2 score of 0, and higher is better.

The competition's data tab states: leaderboard scores … are not meaningful.

You would never evaluate a model on its training data, would you? For the time being, the public leaderboard evaluates models on a subset of the training data; this leads to inflated scores and favors overfitted models.


You don't believe me yet? Go find these two lines in your notebook:

enet_model = (...)
enet_model(X_train, y_train)


or 

xgb_model = xgb.XGBRegressor
xgb_model.fit


Just insert the following lines and look at the output:

from sklearn.model_selection import cross_val_score

enet_model = ElasticNet(...)
enet_score = cross_val_score(enet_model, X_train, y_train).() # R2 
('CV R2 :', enet_score)
enet_model.(X_train, y_train)
(enet_model.coef_)

xgb_model = xgb.XGBRegressor(...)
xgb_score = cross_val_score(xgb_model, X_train, y_train).() # R2 
('CV R2 :', xgb_score)
xgb_model.(X_train, y_train)


If the R2 scores are negative or the ElasticNet coefficients are all zero, delete the notebook as quickly as possible!

Interestingly, @laurentlanteigne's  original Hull Starter Notebook has a positive R2 score and a realistic Sharpe ratio.

Call to action:



Implement cross-validation for all your models.

If you want to learn from other people's notebooks, ignore those with high leaderboard scores and study the others.
