Model and online learning
LGBM models with l1 loss and same hyperparameters for both consumption and production models, including 10,000 num_estimators during training.

I implemented a strategy of online learning, retraining the model every 7 days for consumption and every 4 days for production.
For final prediction phase, I start from 1st of February and don't use any pre-trained models.

Online learning data
I assign data_block_id to the new data, and keep the last 400 data block_ids history for each iteration - to use with online learning.

Consumption models
For consumption, I ensemble two LGBM models: one absolute and one relative. The absolute model was trained with the target as-is, while the relative model was trained with the formula (target - target_mean) / (target_mean + 1e-7), where target_mean is the weekly average for the specific combination of county, is_business, product_type andhour of day.
The weights were set at 0.6/0.4 for relative vs. absolute.

Production models
Forr production, I ensemble two LGBM models with different seeds, both trained with target/installed_capacity. Weights for production models were set at 0.5/0.5.

Cross-validation & Leaderboard (LB) score
After learning that the forecasting test period would run from Feb-2024 to Apr-2024 (from post), I decided to use the three months from Feb-2023 to Apr-2023 as validation and the LB score to guide me in my decisions.

The training procedure is typical for time series, where I take one month, for example, Feb-2024, as the validation set and as training set all data prior to that month. If I observe an improvement in a particular feature across all three months, I choose to retain that feature.

For online learning, I only relied on the LB to choose the suitable ensemble and determine the num_iterations for the models and monitored time of submission. Increasing the num_iterations helps, but comes at the cost of submission time.

For submissions, I utilized two notebook templates—one for consumption and another for production. As some of you may have noticed, I climbed to the 3rd place out of silver position on the last day. This success didn't come as a surprise to me because I was already aware of my LB score in advance, calculated by the combined LB scores of consumption and production. Consequently, all I had to do was merge them for the final submissions.

Features
I collected distinct sets of features for consumption and production, guided by cross-validation results. The outcome was 90 features for consumption and 77 features for production.

These features are primarily of type forecast weather, time-related features (such as hour and dayofweek), and lag features.

  • The base features of forecast weather are similar those used in public notebooks i.e. datetime forecast averages per county.

  • Interestingly, the time-related features differ between consumption and production. For instance, consumption features included month and week, while production excludes both.

  • lag features, are the target and daily target percent changes that span from 2 to 14 days, and add the weekly averages such as target_mean of last 7 days.
    Consumption model also used the lag production that is divided by installed_capacity. Assumption here is that the production of energy has an influence on the consumption behavior but not vice versa.

  • is_holiday flag and I adjusted the dayofweek during holidays to 7 (representing Sunday). This adjustment was made because the target behavior during holidays closely resembles that of weekends.

I opted not to use gas/electricity prices and historical weather data, they did not prove to be beneficial during cross-validation.

What didn't work for me

  • Sequential GRU models trained on daily data of 24 hours

  • Ensembling with XGBoost and CatBoost

  • Post-processing the targets based on the absolute error of the lag targets was unsuccessful for me

  • Fitting power law functions that can capture the relation between target and surface_solar_radiation_downwards

  • In the final two days, I focused on developing different models for most influential (county, is_business, product_type) pairs. Here I used the exact values of the individual forecast weather points for a county, instead of averaging. I hypothesized that it would provide more variability to the ensemble. Although this approach yielded a slight improvement, it was not as much as I expected, and unfortunately, time constraints prevented further optimization.
    Looking backwards I should have done a model for each county and not for each county, is_business, product_type
