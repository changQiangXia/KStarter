Strongest features on top of the public notebook:



Difference in forecasted weather vs forecasted 2 days ago. This was very interesting for me to observe - you need to use differences in the forecasts, and not the difference between forecasts and actuals, because weather forecasts are ALWAYS so much higher than actuals. I don't know who is in charge of those weather forecasts but they should do a better job.

Various target transformations (for example I noticed that it took a long time for my model to adjust to a "shock" - a situation where the target has a significant level shift - so I did lots of feature engineering to help the model recognize that fact)


Additionally:



Setting min_samples_in_leaf = 1 in LGBM. This normally leads to overfitting but can be considered also as an automatic outlier removal. The latter outweighed the former in this competition.

Retraining strategy

This should be very important for this competition as the data for production is very non-stationary due to the constant increase of installed capacity.

Handling non-stationarity

We all love Boosting (LGBM, XGB, Catboost), but if there is one nemesis for Boosting it is non-stationarity: the learned function can never predict outside of seen target ranges. It is very important in real-life production ML settings and Kudos to Kaggle and Hosts for giving us a competition with such a challenge.

Various approaches can be used to overcome that. In this competition, a great approach was shown in the public notebook by @vitalykudelya: 



Predict the delta vs strong baseline (in our case target_48h)


An additional approach that worked for me:



Training the model based on data inflated by 10% (but 20% was already too much)


And of course the model retraining should help.

An interesting characteristic of LGBM is that when you train the model using all segments, the interleaving of targets reduces the problem of non-stationarity, and the only segments where we really have a problem are the largest ones.
