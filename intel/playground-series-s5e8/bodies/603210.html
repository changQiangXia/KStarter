And now for a few details …….


Around the beginning of the month, I noticed that I'd come 5th in PSS5E2, PSS5E4 and PSS5E6 (with great variance in the other months), and joked to myself that if this tiny sample were predictive, I'll come 5th again - well aware that it was unlikely to be the case, and there were way, way more ways for that to change in the wrong direction but only 4 ways in the right direction. I was busy with other things, and started slow, with AutoML and a few simple models plus public notebooks. A few things were clear early on: the CV-LB correspondence was pretty good, XGB and LGBM seemed to be doing a bit better than CatBoost, and while the gains of adding new models were sort of tapering off, I was still getting consistent improvements in both CV and LB. Autogluon (AG) was outperforming Ridge, Hill Climbing and LGBM in ensembling. As with most things AG, the longer you ran the notebook for, the better the scores were, so I started running AG for as close to 12 hours as possible (due to the Kaggle limit). This huge time requirement was a bit annoying, especially with an eye towards a likely close finale, but I didn't worry much about it as there was still a lot of time left. Oh, and the good old flippin' trick was working as usual in a playground competition on binary classification - it produced an improvement of up to 0.00001, and thus would be expected to boost the private LB scores up to 0.00003. So I started flipping the results routinely - why leave a 'free' improvement on the table? All in all, everything seemed nicely set up for a good few weeks.


Around this time, I really got stuck into the competition, adding OOFs regularly and usually with some benefit to the CV and LB. In the previous phase, I took out several OOFs after there was no improvement with Ridge - but after noticing that AG was generally producing the best ensemble scores, I started keeping OOFs as long as at least AG benefited from it. I did dabble around a bit, trying a few approaches to prune the OOF collection, but never for long. I did inspect the feature importance reported by AG, and at one point, reran a notebook after removing all OOFs with a score below an arbitrary threshold - this reduced the CV slightly, but improved the LB. Food for thought? But I didn't fancy the idea of taking two days to do two such runs, so I toyed with the idea of a 15 minute run followed by filtering some OOFs and then another 11 hours run within the same notebook, but never got around to doing it. On the other hand, as the OOF collection grew, 11-hour runs of AG began exceeding 12 overall, and crashing.

I did try out a few new things. Notably, thanks to @siukeitin, @tilii7 and @mahoganybuttstrings, I finally got around to trying out xLearn - I was impressed by the speed as well as performance of the models, but they didn't seem to improve my ensemble's CV, so I didn't spend much further time on them - but I think I'll keep going back to them. As usual, I employed various tweaks to generate new OOFs - playing around with hyperparameters, folds, model diversity, inclusion or exclusion of the original dataset, feature engineering, etc. etc. I also began moving up the leaderboard, catching up with @masayakawamata and others. Based on experience, I did expect to hit a wall soon, but didn't seem to hit one until past 70 OOFs, way beyond what @tilii7 had reported - guess I was building a larger collection of weaker models 😀

So far, most of my code had been run on Kaggle, but on whim I reran a 9-model ensemble from an earlier competition - it took forever, but that was kinda ok because nobody killed it after 12 hours. Running with and without the original produced 18 new OOFs "on the cheap", and also demonstrated that it was quite straightforward to get 0.97x with solo GBDTs and 0.974x with just 9 OOFs, even without any competition-specific tuning, target encoding etc. Moreover, the results underscored the value of weak models which boost diversity - models like Random Forest and K-Nearest Neighbors helped, as did stronger ones like LGBM-GOSS and LGBM-DART. By the 24th, I was in the lead, and up to 100 OOFs. Two consecutive runs of AG on 100 and 102 OOFs both scored 0.97839 - had I hit a wall? I did a 50:50 blend, and flipped it - lo and behold, I hit 0.97842! This felt like a bit of a lottery ticket. Unbeknownst to me, I had already hit scores high enough to win at the end of the month (0.97795, 0.97796 and 0.97799).


The 0.97842 on the public LB proved to be exceptionally hard to beat - as I added more OOFs, the CV kept improving ever so slightly, but most ensembles still failed to score more than 0.97839, and even flipping and ensembling failed to cross 0.97842, though it was matched a few times. Eventually, at 127 OOFs, I hit 0.97840 for the first time with a solo submission, but this blended with the previous 0.97842 yielded only 0.97841 (the private LB turned out to be 0.97801). I decided to go back to the 102 OOF ensemble and only add the highest scoring OOF added after that to get a 103 OOF ensemble, and another with 7 other OOFs I though might help, to get 110. when I blended and flipped these ensembles of 103 and 110 OOFs with individual LB scores of 0.97839 and 0.97840, I got "only" 0.97841 - but the private LB turned out to be 0.97803, higher than the two submissions that I did choose. With the last day looming, I was still stuck at 0.97842 on the public LB, still with a clear lead but with others gradually narrowing the gap.

While part of me thought a lead of 0.00014 might be enough to retain the no. 1 position on the private LB, the rest of me didn't want to stop, even after 5-6 days of failing to improve my best LB score. I suddenly recalled that while a rerun of AG (not as ensemble) after adding some feature engineering (FE) had improved the ensemble, I hadn't rerun RF and CatBoost with FE, even though they'd earlier helped the ensemble. So I reran a few models with FE, and submitted a 136 OOF ensemble to run with AG overnight - this ended up scoring 0.97844 (without flipping), finally yielding a breakthrough and my highest score on the public LB. I could have gone to sleep after submitting this late at night, but this was when the real madness commenced.

10 months ago, @hardyxu52 had taught us that CatBoost could be fed the predictions of another model as a baseline, and it would then potentially produce better predictions. In that particular competition, CatBoost was King, and ended up improving all models that @hardyxu52 fed it. It's an ode to my monumental laziness that it only too me 10 months to get around to finally seriously attempt it, and that too on the last night of the competition. I "vibe-coded" with Google AI mode, finally seeming to get it right, but not getting any improvement in several high-scoring models that I tried. Frustrated, I tried the same with LGBM (it turns out that you can do this with LGBM and XGB as well, and indeed @cdeotte had reminded us of this wrt XGB a few days earlier, in his post on final tips) - again, to no avail. Finally, I decided to try with a slightly lower-scoring CatBoost model, and what did you know - I finally got an improvement! Excited, I tried LGBM and XGB with the same baseline, and they all produced a better CV! Due to doing this so late, I could not waste any submissions to check whether they also improved LB - I just had to take a hop of faith, since the CV-LB correspondence was so good. Over the next few hours, I tried over a dozen OOFs - unfortunately, only a fe showed improvement, and I was running out of time. After all, to finish in time, I did need to start the AG run by 7:30 AM or so, due to which I added 7 OOFs generated from 4 existing OOFs, all of which were improved by CatBoost, but only 2 by LGBM and
1 by XGB (I may of course have made mistakes with LGBM and/or XGB). Unfortunately, this last throw of the dice only produced 0.97835 (private: 0.97799), so I didn't choose it - but I'd learned some new stuff, so it was worthwhile, though I should probably not leave it till last night next time. There was also some satisfaction in noting that Hill Climbing chose as many as 4 of these in a 19-model ensemble. In the end, the 136 OOF ensemble with 0.97844 scored on 0.97798 on the private LB, and its flipped version (one of my two choices) scored 0.97801, my official final score. Looking back, I had over 20 scores of over 0.97794, the first few coming over a week before the end - this bit was particularly reminiscent of my results in PSS4E8, exactly a year ago. I've been no. on the public LB several times before slipping away on the private LB - so it was a great feeling to hold on to the no. 1 once the private LB was revealed 😀

Epilogue: Autogluon was special, but not unbeatable After a certain point, I began to wonder whether AG was overfitting, as it was outscoring the other ensemblers by a lot - usually, the margins are much smaller. I meant to try out an L3 ensemble of GBDTs and NNs of the kind @masayakawamata had reported a few times (and many have reported an XGB-NN blend, including @cdeotte in a few playground competitions), but didn't get around to completing it in time. After the deadline, I finally implemented an NN ensembler with 5-fold CV, and tested it on the final 143-OOF ensemble, which scored 0.97835/0.97798. It surprised me by scoring 0.97829/0.97800 - and when used in a Hill Climbing ensemble along with LGBM and Logistic Regression ensemblers (along with flipping), the result was a stunning 0.97841/0.97805, better than any of my results with AG. All at a fraction of the cost in time (but a higher GPU cost, since the NN took nearly an hour of GPU time, while I ran AG on CPU). At less than 1.5 hours, it took about 8-fold less time than an AG run, and produced better results. I really must do this from now on. Earlier adoption of this approach along with using Optuna and pseudolabels - both standard approaches that I didn't get around to including - would have probably brought scores of 0.97850+/0.97810+ within reach.

I'm not sure who's reading this novel of a writeup, that too 4 days after the competition ended (or perhaps you've landed here at some distant point in the future), but thanks for your time, and I hope it wasn't wasted. Happy Kaggling!
