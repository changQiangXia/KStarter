Hey everyone 👋,

I'm excited to join this competition and put to the test all the experience I’ve gained so far. Unfortunately, in some of the previous quantitative finance competitions, I struggled during the forecasting phase — mainly because the API submission process wasn’t clearly explained. This led to failures or subpar performance in my notebooks, even though I had solid models during training.

That’s why I’m really hoping this competition will be different — clearer, fairer, and more collaborative. I’ve compiled some key questions I believe are crucial not just for me but for many participants, especially those building real-time or hybrid models.



❓ Forecasting Phase Questions:




424 Targets per Time Step:



We are expected to predict 424 targets at every time_id.
→ Is the inference per timestep truly required to be completed in under 1 minute, including data preprocessing, feature engineering, and inference?


Train Set Availability:



During the forecasting phase, will the train set continue to update with each timestep, or will it remain fixed?

Are we allowed to maintain internal state or cache features across timesteps?


Label Lags Interpretation:



The provided label lags (t-1, t-2,t-3, t-4,…) contain missing values.
→ Should we expect similar missing patterns during forecasting?
→ How large is the typical gap between the lags and the actual target (in terms of timesteps)?


Model Feasibility:




It's very difficult to run 424 separate univariate models within 1 minute per timestep.


And can we load models once globally, or must they be loaded fresh within the prediction window?


Inference Input Structure:



During the real inference stage, will we receive a single row at a time, or a batch of rows?
This impacts whether we can batch our predictions or not.


Warm-up Period:



Will we get a short warm-up period at the beginning of test time to initialize our models?


Evaluation Metric:



Are all 424 targets equally weighted in the final evaluation, or are some targets prioritized?


Lag Labels as Real-World Simulation:



Are the lagged labels designed to simulate delayed real-world signals (e.g., from a reporting system), or are they purely synthetic for model training?




Please, if any organizers or experienced participants have insights, sharing answers to these will help all of us design fair, realistic, and efficient models. Also, if I missed a question you believe is important, feel free to reply here — the more clarity we get now, the better the competition will be for everyone!

Thanks 🙏 and good luck to all participants!
