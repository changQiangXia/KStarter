Topic #429278: Dimensionality reduction: 5 dimensions are enough
  Author: AmbrosM
  Posted: 2023-08-04 19:57:49.884000
  Votes: 78  Comments: 22

In this competition we are predicting 497 independent time series. Independent??? Did you check the independence of the targets? Everybody looks at correlations between features, but who did look at correlations among the 497 targets?

A PCA or an SVD of the 497 emission time series reveals that five components suffice to explain 100 % of the variance:



This is quite remarkable: It means that the emissions of the 495 locations all are linear combinations of the emissions of only five basis locations!

We can project the time series of the 495 locations to the first two principal components and mark four extreme locations:



The four marked locations are indeed special:



Nyamasheke has the highest average emission and a pattern which differs from every other location.

Lake Kivu has high peaks every January and February.

Kihanga has no seasonal peaks.

Musanze has seasonal peaks every May and October.




The time series of all other locations are linear combinations of these four plus a fifth basis location.

Insight:



We don't need to predict 497 independent time series for 2022. It suffices to predict 5 basis time series, and all others can be derived.

The huge dimensionality reduction (100 % explained variance ratio with only five components) wouldn't be possible if the measurements came from real sensors. It indicates that the emission data has been generated artificially.

I don't yet know how the information about the dimensionality reduction will help improve leaderboard scores, but I'm sure it will… 😉
