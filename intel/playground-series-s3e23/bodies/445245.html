We recently have had playground competitions with huge shakeups. This competition will be different: The dataset is benign and solid work will lead to victory.

The main steps to victory are:

  1. Choose and implement a solid cross-validation strategy
  2. Implement at least the following seven classifiers
    • RandomForestClassifier
    • ExtraTreesClassifier
    • HistGradientBoostingClassifier
    • XGBClassifier
    • LGBMClassifier
    • CatBoostClassifier
    • one non-tree model (the best one you can find), for instance a kernel approximation classifier
  3. Tune the preprocessing and all hyperparameters of every single classifier for maximum auc score in cross-validation
  4. Implement an ensemble of all these classifiers
  5. Tune the ensemble weights for maximum auc score in cross-validation
  6. Compute the test predictions and submit

Background for step 1 (cross-validation): During the three weeks of this competition, you'll evaluate hundreds of models and hyperparameter settings. Cross-validation is important because (a) cv scores are more precise than scores based on a simple train-test split, (b) cv scores are more precise than leaderboard scores and you need precise data to take the right decisions, and (c) you can't evaluate hundreds of models in 21 days with only 5 submissions per day. You'll most probably use a StratifiedKFold or a RepeatedStratifiedKFold in this competition. If you see a public notebook without cross-validation, ignore it. If you see a public notebook with a low cross-validation score, ignore it.

Background for step 2 (classifiers): Kaggle competitions are usually won by an ensemble of models. The more diverse the ensemble, the better. The seven classifiers on the list have been chosen because they give good scores for the competition dataset. Six of them are based on decision trees, but an ensemble created only from decision trees isn't diverse enough. My current favorite for the the seventh model (the non-tree model) is logistic regression with Nyström kernel approximation. It is the best non-decision-tree model I could find, and it is competitive (see bar chart). Sample code for the kernel approximation is in the EDA which makes sense.

Background for step 3 (preprocessing and hyperparameter tuning): All these classifiers have hyperparameters, and the default settings are never good enough for a Kaggle competition. Read the documentation to understand the hyperparameters. Some of the classifiers need preprocessing such as log-transformation, feature engineering, feature selection or scaling, and not every classifier requires the same preprocessing. Experiment with various preprocessing methods to see how they affect the score.

Background for step 5 (ensemble weight tuning): Ensemble weights need to be tuned like every other hyperparameter. And don't tune them for the public leaderboard (although it's tempting), but for cross-validation!

If you follow all steps of these instructions, you'll have a good chance of ending up among the top 10 % of the private leaderboard. And even if you follow only a part of the steps, you'll gain a lot of useful experience.

Good luck!
