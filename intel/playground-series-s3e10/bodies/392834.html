"Pulsars are neutron stars that emit rapid, periodic pulses of radiation. A neutron star is an extremely compact ball of neutrons created from the central core of a star that collapsed under gravity during a supernova explosion."

"Pulsars, in turn, are neutron stars with very intense magnetic fields, which rotate at very high frequencies and emit radiation. Thus, all pulsars are neutron stars, but not all neutron stars are pulsars."

"The neutron star retains much of its original angular momentum, and since it has a much smaller radius than its parent, it forms with a high rate of rotation. … This causes the beam to be visible once for every rotation of the neutron star, leading to the “pulsating” nature of its appearance."

"Quasars are very particular types of galaxy nuclei, they are the so-called "active nuclei" whose emitted light is not the sum of the lights of the stars that make up the nucleus itself. … Pulsars, on the other hand, are neutron stars rotating at a high rotational speed, which emit radio pulses (hence their name)."

"Planetary nebulae are formed when a star that has between 0,8 and 8 solar masses (M⊙) runs out of its nuclear fuel. Above the limit of 8 M⊙ the star would explode, originating a supernova."

"Quasars are the biggest energy emitters in the Universe. A single quasar can achieve luminosities thousands of times greater than that of a galaxy."
