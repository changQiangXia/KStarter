Our solution is a blend of some variants of the following two-branch network:



How did we arrive at this solution? The introductory text of the competition gives a hint: … includes a number of different feature interactions. This competition is an opportunity to explore various methods for identifying and exploiting these feature interactions. Few people knew what to do with this hint. Fortunately, @sudalairajkumar showed us how to identify the feature interactions with Xgbfir. He even produced a dataset listing all interactions found by the tool. 

On the back of an envelope, I constructed a graph of these interactions. The graph had two connected components:



The key idea then was that we needed a network which restricted feature interactions to the connected components of the graph (other interactions produce noise). The two-branch network implements this idea. The left branch learns the interactions among ['f_00', 'f_01', 'f_02', 'f_03', 'f_04', 'f_05', 'f_06', 'f_19', 'f_20', 'f_21', 'f_22', 'f_23', 'f_24', 'f_25', 'f_26', 'f_28', 'f_30', 'ch7'], the right branch processes ['f_07', 'f_08', 'f_09', 'f_10', 'f_11', 'f_12', 'f_13', 'f_14', 'f_15', 'f_16', 'f_17', 'f_18', 'f_29', 'ch0', 'ch1', 'ch2', 'ch3', 'ch4', 'ch5', 'ch6', 'ch8', 'ch9', 'unique_characters', 'i_02_21', 'i_05_22', 'i_00_01_26']. Except for the two-branch architecture, our final implementation doesn't differ from the Advanced Keras notebook.

We worked on a decision tree model as well. Restricting the feature interactions with the hyperparameter interaction_constraints=[features_left, features_right] helped LightGBM reach an lb score of 0.99778, but this was not enough to be included in the blend.

Special thanks go to @cabaxiom for introducing the unique_characters feature, to @wti200 for showing how to analyze feature interactions, to @sudalairajkumar for publishing the Xgbfir output, and of course to @pourchot who with great persistence optimized our network architecture.
