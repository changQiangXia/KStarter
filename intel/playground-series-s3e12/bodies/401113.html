When I looked at the 16 most recent public notebooks, wanting to know what cv scores they reported, I was shocked that only three of them cross-validate properly:

  • 3 notebooks didn't report any score at all. They obviously don't validate their models before submitting.
  • 2 notebooks reported only a training score. The training scores look good, but unfortunately they don't measure generalization performance.
  • 8 notebooks reported a validation score based on train_test_split, usually with a test_size of 0.2. This is wrong.
  • Only 3 notebooks cross-validated correctly and reported a validation score based on StratifiedKFold.

Validating models with a simple train_test_split is wrong because with such a small validation set the result depends too much on the random seed. Imagine you want to compare LogisticRegression and RandomForest. Depending on the random_state, LogisticRegression looks either 0.057 better than RandomForest or 0.080 worse:

for random_state in [464, 879]:
    X_tr, X_va, y_tr, y_va = train_test_split(train[['cond', 'calc']],
                                              train[target],
                                              test_size=0.2,
                                              random_state=random_state)
    lr = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(random_state=0))
    lr.fit(X_tr, y_tr)
    lr_score = roc_auc_score(y_va, lr.predict_proba(X_va)[:,1])
    rf = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=400, max_depth=2,
                                min_samples_leaf=13, max_features=2,
                                random_state=0)
    rf.fit(X_tr, y_tr)
    rf_score = roc_auc_score(y_va, rf.predict_proba(X_va)[:,1])
    print(f"random_state={random_state:4}:   lr: {lr_score:6.3f}   "
          f"rf: {rf_score:6.3f}   difference: {lr_score-rf_score:6.3f}")

# random_state= 464:   lr:  0.785   rf:  0.864   difference: -0.080
# random_state= 879:   lr:  0.822   rf:  0.764   difference:  0.057

We're not talking about the fifth decimal digit here - an error of 0.137 brings you from the top of the leaderboard almost to the bottom!

Now imagine that you used the same train_test_split in the objective function of Optuna: The hyperparameters you get from Optuna would be completely random.

In the current competition, you need at least a StratifiedKFold or better a RepeatedStratifiedKFold to get meaningful results because ten folds are not enough.
