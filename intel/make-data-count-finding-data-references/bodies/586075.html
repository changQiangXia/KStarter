I think it's safe to say that the training data for this competition is not fit for purpose. We've not heard from @daniellalowenberg, @jenniferlin15, or @inversion when (or if) new training data will be provided, so I have gone ahead and made my own (!)

I've gone through all the training PDFs and highlighted what I think are the primary and secondary data citations. I've then exported those highlights to a CSV file in the same format as the training data. I don't claim to have got every citation, nor that the distinction between "Primary" and "Secondary" is always clear. There are some glitches in the table regarding formatting and encoding (getting annotations out of PDFs can be tricky), but you may find it usable. The annotated PDFs and the training labels generated fron those annotations are on GitHub.

As an experiment treated the existing training data as predictions of the training data I extracted and computed the F1 score: it is 0.446. Hence the training data provided for the competition is a pretty poor predictor of what I think are the actual citations. Hence I don't think that training on the existing training labels is likely to lead to great results.

Of course, the real question is how accurate are the labels on the hidden test collection? If they are of similar quality to the training data then it is going to be pretty hard to get meaningful scores. I hope the organisers address this as soon as possible.

Update: I've now had a chance to manually clean the training labels I generated from the PDFs (removing encoding errors, glueing together a few split DOIs, etc.). The F1 score for the original training data is now 0.446. Latest version of my training data is in GitHub and also in Kaggle as a dataset https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rdmpage/new-training-labels/.
