How The Game Begins - EDA

When the game begins, our web browser pops out a new window of size 660x880. The following 11 screen shots are the first 11 things we see. We do not have a choice. The game pauses on each image until we click anywhere. 

The first screen we see is the image in the top left (which is also image in yellow box in room tunic.historicalsociety.closet below). No matter where we click, grampa says "Whatcha doing over there, Jo?". Then no matter where we click, we say "Just talking to Teddy.". The dialog continues as pictured below. (Right click the images below and choose "Open Image in New Tab" to enlarge images).



First Game Room - tunic.historicalsociety.closet

Each row of the dataset has 2 pairs of coordinates, (room_coor_x, room_coor_y) and (screen_coor_x, screen_coor_y). The first pair is relative to the black rectangle below. And the second pair is relative to your pop out window (not your computer screen). Two examples of pop up window views are the yellow and blue rectanges below. 

In the room tunic.historicalsociety.closet (i.e. the first room), then -1064&lt;=room_coor_x&lt;=926 and -393&lt;=room_coor_y&lt;=327. Each room has its own bounds. The screen coordinates are always bound by your pop out window size. 95% of users do not resize the pop out window and 0&lt;=room_coor_x&lt;=880 and 0&lt;=room_coor_y&lt;=660

First Action Option

After we finish the mandatory introductory dialog, we are free to choose our first action. The scatter plot below shows where users make their first action. Most users click Teddy (the lovable pet badger). Some (curious users) click the photos on the wall. Some click to the left of the room to navigate (eager to complete their first quest):



First Quest

The last message in the dialog (before Teddy's emoji) is Jo saying, "Now where did I put my notebook?". This is our first quest. We must find the notebook. If we traverse to the far left side of this first room, we see the notebook on the floor. Every user must find and click this notebook to proceed. Below are every users first click finding the notebook. When we hover over the notebook, we see an orange exclamation point. Some users click the exclamation point, and some users click directly on the notebook:



First Use of Notebook

After clicking the notebook, the notebook opens and takes over the full pop up window. The message "Found it!" appears in the bottom of the screen over the notebook. To continue the game, we can click anywhere. Most users click on the words "Found it", but some users click elsewhere. Regardless of where we click, the message disappears and a white X in black circle appears in top right corner. Most users click the white X in black circle which closes the notebook and play continues. Some users click elsewhere and nothing happens. They must keep clicking until they click the white X in black circle. Some users even click the words "Homework Monster" in the bottom center of the notebook!

User Behavior - Feature Engineering

There is so much to learn about the 11k users by just observing their behavior in this opening sequence. How long do they take to read each text dialog before continuing? Where do they click each time to continue the game. Where is their first click (grampa, letter, elsewhere)? When users click grampa, do they click grampa's body or face? When Teddy appears on the screen, how many users start clicking the pet versus clicking on Jo to continue the dialog? Which users click text boxes to continue. Which users resize the initial pop up window, which don't? etc etc.

This is just the opening sequence. There are 19 rooms and 23 levels to explore. Each moment, users take one of 11 actions. There are many dialogs and many quests! Different users click different things. Users reveal much predictive information (i.e. features) by their game playing behavior!
