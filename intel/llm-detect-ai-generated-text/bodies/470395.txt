Thanks to Kaggle, Vanderbilt University, and The Learning Agency Lab for hosting this competition. Detecting AI-generated text has been an important research topic with the rise of LLMs, and I'm sure the methods developed in this competition will contribute to this research area.

Link to inference code: https://www.kaggle.com/code/wowfattie/daigt-2nd-place
Link to training code and data: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/wowfattie/daigtpretraindata

Here is my solution:

I started working on this competition relatively late. At first, I did some finetuning with deberta models on datasets generously shared by other competitors, all my initial trials resulted in nearly perfect CV but much lower and unstable public LB score. After realizing this finetuning would not go anywhere, I started playing with the tfidf kernels and forming ideas to improve it. During this process, James's post on creating large and diverse dataset and training a more general model grabbed my attention, and I immediately realized this could be an important "pretraining" step in a winning solution. So I abandoned my to-do list and invested all my efforts and computing resources to reproduce his approach. In several days I created about half a million human/AI pairs from the SlimPajama dataset with various LLMs. After finetuning on this dataset using deberta-v3-large, public/private LB was at 0.916/0.967. This public score was much lower compared with what James reported, probably due to some difference in generation settings, the LLMs used, or data selection.

After this "pretraining" step, the original "language" model was transformed to a general "human/AI text generation" classification model. Since we know the test prompts are from persuade corpus only, it's worth doing another finetuning to adapt the model to those essays. After looking into the persuade corpus csv, I noticed that all the 5 prompts in test data ask students to write essays based on the contents of some articles, but I could not find those articles anywhere  (other than the Venus one). The issue is if those articles were used for generating the test set by the host, the AI texts could be very similar to human texts. To close this gap, I did language model (LM) finetuning on the student essays so the LLMs could generate texts mimicking students' writing, including citations from the original articles and even typos. To perform the LM finetuning, llm-studio is the best tool, no code was required, just prepared the csv file and clicked on the GUIs, and the models were ready for essay generation. Finetuning the general classifier on this dataset improved the public/private LB to around 0.94/0.98. My final 2nd place kernel contains an ensemble of two deberta-v3-large models and one deberta-large model, I also finetuned the three models on the DAIGT-V4-TRAIN-DATASET and added them in the ensemble, the public/private LB score became 0.967/0.983. My best public score was achieved by blending the above deberta predictions with the 0.961 tfidf kernel, but the private score is lower.
