When I look at feature engineering in public notebooks, I see the same mistakes again and again. Check your notebook for the following six mistakes, and you may be able to improve your score:

  1. Not enough creativity: Several public notebook aggregate sensor values by mean, median, min, max, std and var. This is not enough for the current competition. After mean and std, it's natural to try skewness and kurtosis as well. Skewness is a useless feature, but kurtosis of sensor_04 became my single most important feature.

  2. Duplicate aggregations: Do you aggregate using mean, sum, std and var? Sum is proportional to mean and brings no added value for any estimator. Std is the square root of variance and brings no added value for decision trees (it may be useful for linear estimators, however).

  3. No feature selection: If you give a model too many features, the score deteriorates. In the current competition, reducing the number of features is important. You can use sequential feature selection (my favorite) or permutation feature importance, for instance.

  4. Not using the count of sequences per subject: We have seen in the EDA that we can count sequences per subject, and subjects with more sequences have a higher probability for being in state=1. This is my second most important feature.

  5. Data leak in cross-validation: Don't use KFold or StratifiedKFold! In the current competition, the subjects of train and test are disjoint, and it is important to validate that the model is able to generalize to previously unseen subjects. GroupKFold with groups=train.subject ensures that the validation set consists of subjects which do not appear in train.

  6. RandomForestClassifier: RandomForestClassifier often gives decent results, but in the current competition gradient boosted decision trees are better. You can choose your favorite from HistGradientBoostingClassifier, XGBoost, CatBoost and LightGBM.
