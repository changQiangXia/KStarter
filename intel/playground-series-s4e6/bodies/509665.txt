Hello everyone,

Thanks to Kaggle for the AutoML Grand Prix challenge! Please find my approach write-up as below for this month's challenge-

Overall strategy



Divide the day into 4 parts for 4 diverse experiments and try and submit wisely through the day. Reserve 1 experiment/ submission for the end based on unexpected happenings/ errors

Start off with simple models and then try out complex approaches

Use the complete day for experiments and make best use of available computational resources 


Experiment details

Experiment 1



I started off with my usual baseline model pipeline with and without the original data. I used only the competition data for CV score creation. Adding the original data to the train data helped me at this stage

My initial submission scored 0.83758 and I stood 2nd on the public leaderboard 8 hours into the day


Experiment 2



I tuned my baseline models, switched to GPU settings locally and added-removed models in the ensemble

My second submission scored approximately the same as my first one - perhaps this had no impact on the public LB  despite a CV improvement

I was 14 hours into the day and was off Kaggle for other work (long run and workouts)


Experiment 3



The public kernel by Mr. Gaurav Datta was now available

5-6 participants had the same score and top places were filling up. I found myself at 12th on the LB

I simply built a mode of his kernels (roc_ovo and roc) and my experiments and submitted to score 0.83924 and stood 8th 21 hours into the day

My kernel for this submission is here


Experiment 4



I tried to improve the predictions with my work by trying to blend with more kernels and experiments. Some of these were auto ML solutions and others were manual ML solutions

I ended up scoring around 0.83816 22 hours into the day


Experiment 5



I continued my blending process and blended with some public kernels and my experiments through the day

My score stayed the same at  0.83816 23 hours into the day (local time 3am Sunday, June2)


I finished the day on 0.83924 and worthy of writing this anecdote! 
Thanks to @gauravduttakiit for the kernel and others for their forum contributions!

Now hoping to build some stable models through the month!
