As we know, the Playground Series datasets are synthetically augmented variants of long-standing popular tabular datasets, and this month was no exception.

I am a firm believer in the power of feature engineering to find extra predictive power in datasets, and in view of this a good place for inspiration is to review the work of other people on the 'unadulterated' dataset. However, my off-line tests steadfastly refused to show better results when creating new features such as cross-terms, interaction terms etc. even for features that have shown strong promise on the original dataset.

Another route is to remove very weak features that are essentially noise, but that too degraded my baseline score. Based on the finding of last months' challenge, where the dataset was trivial but the color of the synthetic noise had some weak predictive power my hypothesis is that even useless features became predictive features due to this color, hence dropping them degrades performance.

Also, depending on which features to treat as categorical or not had strange effects (in the end I gave up exploring and went with the "AmbrosM" list). I was left wondering if the categorical data is actually augmented as categorical data or not.

In short; everything I tried in the time available was fruitless. However, as we all know Gaurav Dutta created and published a very strong Public LB notebook (whether it is a actually robust solution or not will have to wait for the Private LB…)

In the end, with time running short, I blended his output file with a CatBoost baseline and a LightGBM baseline by simply taking the mode of the class predictions: my 'best-of-five' was notebook #2, which only blended CatBoost and gave a Public LB score of 0.83875. (Note: taking the mode of just two values is somewhat ill defined…)

To use up my very last submission of the day, just for fun, I changed the random_seed from 42 to 24, and upped the number of CatBoost estimators form 1000 to 1001, and my Public LB score dropped to 0.83679. With this in mind I am inclined to suspect that there was a fair degree of chance/luck at play; …there is nothing quite like combining the perfect storm of synthetic data with the accuracy score metric to make things "interesting"!
