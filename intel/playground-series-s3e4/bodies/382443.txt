Another fun installment of the Playground Series has concluded!

The data was very noisy and the target variable was highly unbalanced (roughly 99.8% negative, 0.02% positive). For my solution I used @samir95's cross validation strategy which seemed to work very well.

Adversarial validation notebooks by @bcruise, @craigmthomas, and @kaggleqrdl helped to sniff out the large differences between the train, test, and original datasets. Because of the large difference between each of the datasets, it was a difficult decision whether to add the original or a subset of the original data to the training data. I did end up using the original data but only a small subset original.query("Class == 1 and Time &gt; 120580") which was only 132 rows. For the reasoning behind Time &gt; 120580 let's take a look at this plot by @satoshiss:



Blue is the training data and red is the test data. The training data provided contained no entries with Time &gt; 120580 so I believed adding this would help the model to generalize better to the test set.

My final best scoring submission ended up being a single Catboost model with no hyper parameter tuning (notebook here). I would really like to hear about the model(s) that you used and their performance. That's all from me for now, see you in the next episode!
