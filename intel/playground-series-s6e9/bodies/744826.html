Part 2: My Workflow

2.1 - Week 1 - Created 200+ Models, Diverse but Meaningless

I used XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM, RealMLP, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression with many different feature groups.

  • Best Single XGB — OOF: 0.94620
  • 200+ Models — OOF: 0.94630 | Too Weak to Submit

The strange thing was that the stronger model beat the weaker one almost everywhere, so the weaker models had nothing left to add.

Blending added almost nothing: my models looked diverse, but they were all learning the same information.

2.2 - Week 2 - Borrowed 10 Public Models

Since I could not build a clearly stronger model, I borrowed around 10 public models.

For each notebook, I built XGB, LGB, CAT, LR, and RealMLP versions, and used Optuna to search for better hyperparameters.

  • Best Single XGB — OOF: 0.94630
  • 300+ Models — OOF: 0.94640 | Public: 0.94652 | Private: 0.94543

At this point, I knew something was wrong, and I needed a stronger single model.

2.3 - Week 3–4 - Trying to Build a Strong Single Model

No matter what I changed, I could not build a single model stronger than 0.94640 OOF.

I almost gave up.

2.4 - The Last 3 Days

  • I asked Claude to generate 100+ features based on @heuljax's ideas and grouped them into 10+ feature sets.
  • I asked ChatGPT to expand Claude's ideas, then sent them back to Claude to generate features as comprehensively as possible.
  • I used forward selection and backward selection to find several good feature combinations.
  • I used Optuna to search for hyperparameters for each combination.

Results:

  • Best Single XGB — OOF: 0.94666
  • Best Single LR — OOF: 0.94661
  • Logreg Blender — OOF: 0.94675 | Public: 0.94683 | Private: 0.94574
  • Hill Climber — OOF: 0.94681 | Public: 0.94684 | Private: 0.94579

Time was so limited that I did not even have time to fully understand what Claude created or why it worked.

Even if I removed all the models from my first four weeks, I could still get a very similar final result.

Part 3: My Learning

3.1 - How to Select the Best Submission?

This is probably the fourth or fifth time I failed to select my best final submission.

  • In S6E3 and S6E5, my Hill Climbing was worse than Logistic Regression, but I chose HC.
  • This time, my Logistic Regression was worse than Hill Climbing, but I chose Logreg.

There is probably no universal rule, so I need a more scientific way to compare final submissions instead of relying on one CV score or one blending method.

3.2 - How to Use LLMs?

I still do not know how to work with LLMs well.

  • Claude told me more than 10 times: "This road is probably a dead end." This made me misjudge the situation.
  • I pasted Claude's answer into ChatGPT and asked ChatGPT to challenge it. This worked much better.

No matter how strong AI agents become, they still need someone to lead them in the right direction.

That part is hard. A weak commander cannot lead a successful army.

Part 4: My Advice

4.1 - Do Not Blind Blend If You Care About the Final Result

Blind blending a few models is not that harmful if your candidates are honest.

But building new blenders based on previous blind-blend results is dangerous.

It can become noise on top of noise.

4.2 - Do Not Make Big Conclusions Too Early

I tried residual analysis and failed, and Claude firmly told me it was a dead end.

  • But most of my final improvement came from a different residual structure.

I tried token features on income and failed, and Claude again firmly told me it was a dead end.

  • But my biggest single improvement came from a different type of token feature.

I started to believe that the Public LB ceiling for this competition was around 0.946.

  • In the end, many teams broke 0.947 on the Public LB.
