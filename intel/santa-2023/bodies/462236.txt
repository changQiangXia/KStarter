Welcome to Santa 2023! I'm hoping to have a getting started notebook up within a few days that introduces the competition more fully, but for now I wanted to sketch out a few notes.

There are three main puzzle types: cube, wreath, and globe.

Cubes

cube puzzles are like Rubik's cubes. A cube_2/2/2 puzzle has its faces laid out like this:

         Positions                                Solution State

         +--------+                               +--------+
         ||||
         ||||
         ||||
+--------+--------+--------+--------+    +--------+--------+--------+--------+
||||||||||
||||||||||
||||||||||
+--------+--------+--------+--------+    +--------+--------+--------+--------+
         ||||
         ||||
         ||||
         +--------+                               +--------+


The positions are what are changed by the permutations (moves) given in puzzle_info.csv. Each position is assigned a color that can change by the action of a permutation. The puzzle starts out in an initial_state and must be permuted through a sequence of moves to its solution_state.

A wreath puzzle is two rings joined at two points, roughly like this:

               
                   
                   
             


There are usually points between the common points, though.

Globes

A globe puzzle is a sphere with cuts along lines of latitude and longitude. If you poke a hole at the North and South poles, cut along the meridian, and spread it out, it will look like a grid:
