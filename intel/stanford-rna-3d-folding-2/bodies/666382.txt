Topic #666382: Welcome to Part 2 of the Stanford RNA 3D Folding Challenge!
  Author: Rhiju Das
  Posted: 2026-01-06 18:01:23.320000
  Votes: 35  Comments: 35

In 2025, in Part 1 of this competition, Kagglers took on the fundamental problem of RNA 3D structure prediction.

RNA chains are the basis for new medicines and the oldest forms of life — and the most important RNAs fold up into beautiful three-dimensional structures, which underlie their functions.

Unfortunately, the world's efforts to advance biology and biotechnology have been slowed down by our inability to computationally predict these RNA 3D structures.

In 2025, Kaggle teams tackled this problem and achieved automated RNA 3D structure prediction methods that, for the first time, tied with a top human expert group!

A big surprise was the revival of template-based modeling, a non-deep-learning approach. Check out our summary paper at Template-based RNA structure prediction advanced through a blind code competition.

Part 1 focused on single RNA chains under 1000 nts forming largely single structures. 

Part 2 now adds more  use cases important for biology and biotechnology:



Single chain RNAs whose structures are different from previously available templates.

RNA complexes with multiple RNA chains

RNA 'machines' with multiple states - try to capture each of the states in your five predictions.

RNAs whose conformations depend on proteins, other nucleic acids, and/or small molecule ligands.

RNA systems with sizes up to 5500 nucleotides.


We've also updated the evaluation metric to be more stringent. The TM-score metric now only counts residues in the prediction and ground truth as ‘aligned’ if the residues have matching residue numbers.

Last, we've updated our baseline. In Part 1, for each target, the best-of-Kaggle prediction was similar or just barely better than the best publicly available prior 3D template, as discovered by an oracle with knowledge of the ground truth. This time, we’re making the best_template_oracle score available on the Public leaderboard (0.554). 

There's a lot we're curious about:



How hard will it be to generalize Part 1's notebooks to multiple chains or proteins?

How long will it take to beat the best template oracle on the Public leaderboard? Will top models generalize to the Private leaderboard?

Will literature-aware LLMs that read the English language description fields be able to find templates missed last time?

Will Kaggle notebooks be able to leverage chemical mapping profiles for millions of RNAs, available in the 2024 Ribonanza Kaggle competition train and test data?

The maximum possible mean TM-score allowed by the inherent flexibility of these RNA targets is about 0.8. Can codes achieve this milestone in the RNA structure prediction problem? 

Can a new generation of agentic AI approaches and problem solvers –&nbsp;here receiving feedback from the Public leaderboard – accelerate research in Part 2?


We’re looking forward to seeing what Kagglers come up with. We welcome back all who participated in Part 1 – your notebooks should be submittable to Part 2 with few or no updates. And we are excited to meet Kagglers just approaching RNA for the first time -- the fresh insights and high-placing models of teams with a "beginner's mindset" have had a huge impact in prior RNA competitions.
