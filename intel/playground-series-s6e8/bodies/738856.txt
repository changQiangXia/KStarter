Part 2: My Workflow

2.1 - Week 1 - Create 100–200 Basic Models

OOF: 0.97013 | Public: 0.97120 | Private: 0.97096

I started with XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, RealMLP, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest.

Each notebook usually generated around 20–30 models, and most models were simply:



RAW + One Feature Group + Original Data


For example:



RAW + RAW_TE + Original Data as COL

RAW + Ratio + Original Data as COL

RAW + Digit + Original Data as ROW

RAW + Round + Original Data as ROW

RAW + Bins + No Original Data

RAW + Allcat + No Original Data

…


2.2 - Week 2 - Improve the Basic Models

OOF: 0.97035 | Public: 0.97134 | Private: 0.97109



For CatBoost and RealMLP, I added RAW features as Object.

For XGBoost and LightGBM, I added RAW_TE features to the pipelines.


This generated another 100–200 stronger models, and at this point, I still did no manual feature engineering.

2.3 - Week 3 - Feature Engineering

OOF: 0.97052 | Public: 0.97149 | Private: 0.97120

I created my first useful manual feature:



fake_daily = social + work + game


It worked. The idea was simple: if daily is missing, fake_daily may recover part of that information.

Then I created:



fake_social = daily - work - game

fake_work = daily - social - game

fake_game = daily - social - work


I treated these features as RAW features and applied simple Ratio, Bins, TE, and other transformations.

It became relatively easy to get around 0.969 OOF and 0.970+ LB with XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and RealMLP.

2.4 - Week 4 - Nothing Works

Final OOF: 0.97055 | Public: 0.97148 | Private: 0.97123

I thought my problem was diversity and blending, so I spent a lot of time building different models and testing different blenders, but almost nothing helped.

Looking back, I should have spent more time improving one strong XGB model.

I thought I needed a better blender. In fact, I needed a better model.



Part 3: My Learning

3.1 - Public Notebooks Are More Useful Than I Thought

In the past, I did not like using public notebooks because I could not fully control or understand them.

This time, I used more than 200 public predictions, and a blend of them performed better than a blend of my own models.

So I learned that:



No matter how much I tried to diversify my models, many of them were still similar.

Public predictions gave me more real diversity.


So I want to thank all the original authors and everyone who collected and organized these predictions.

Of course, using public predictions is still a gamble, but completely ignoring them can also be a mistake.

3.2 - I Stopped Improving Strong Models Too Early

When Chris moved far ahead, I thought he must be using some special blending methods.

I tried:



XGBoost, CatBoost, and RealMLP as blenders

Splitting data by missing counts and blending separately

Adding around 40 different NN models

Many other diversity ideas


I was wrong, and I should have spent more time on feature engineering and improving my strongest models.

3.3 - It Is Time to Learn New Things

After reading Chris's solution, I realized there are still many new things I need to learn, especially vibe coding and AI agents.

Honestly, I have almost no idea how to use them now, but they look really powerful.



Part 4: My Advice

I want to share a few methods that worked for me.

4.1 - Practice Old Competitions

If you are still struggling to reach the Top 500, do not just jump into new competitions. Try finished competitions first.

For AUC competitions, you can practice on: S5E8, S5E11, S6E3, S6E5.

This helps you understand:



What kinds of feature engineering are repeatedly useful

Which blending methods are more stable

How OOF, Public LB, and Private LB are related


Once you have your own stable pipeline, you can have a much better start in a new competition.

4.2 - Make Sure Your Model Is Good Enough

If someone has already shown that LR can reach around 0.960 and XGB/LGB can reach around 0.966 without much feature engineering, but your tuned model is still far below that, your pipeline probably has a problem.

Small changes may not help much, and restarting with a simple baseline may be more useful.

4.3 - Keep Your Experiments Simple

I prefer to do one main thing in one notebook:



One for different feature engineering

One for Optuna search

One for different seeds / folds / learning rates

One for testing a new idea


I do not put everything into one notebook, and I do not draw a conclusion before trying different combinations.

4.4 - Never Tune for Public LB

You will often see people jump 100 ranks or drop 100 ranks in a row. This happens in AUC competitions.

For example:



Suppose my good model has a true score of 0.97125.

By blind blending on Public LB, I may find one submission at 0.97130 and suddenly jump 100 ranks.

But the true performance of that submission could be only 0.97120 because I overfit the Public LB.

Another model with a true 0.97125 may finally beat me on the Private LB, even if its owner does nothing.


So I mainly trust my OOF, and Public LB is just a check of whether my OOF improvement also behaves reasonably on the test data.
