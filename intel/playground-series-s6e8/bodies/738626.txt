Solution overview

The final pipeline had four stages:



Start from the disclosed 205-model OOF Rank-Logit-Regime fusion by
Byer/HBoyang.

Convert both meta-level prediction inputs to strict percentile ranks before
blending, using the same representation for OOF validation and test
inference.

Replace one global blend weight with OOF-selected weights conditioned on
prediction-risk quantiles. I trained independent 8-, 10-, and 12-bin
variants and rank-averaged their outputs.

Correct two test rows whose complete feature vectors exactly duplicated one
labeled training row with an unambiguous target.


The final submitted prediction can be summarized as:

base rank + fusion rank
        -&gt; risk-conditional blend (8 bins)
        -&gt; risk-conditional blend (10 bins)
        -&gt; risk-conditional blend (12 bins)
        -&gt; equal rank ensemble
        -&gt; exact-duplicate correction


Public foundation and attribution

The main predictive strength came from the public 205-member OOF fusion in:



Byer/HBoyang, S6E8 Rank-Logit-Regime Fusion:
https://www.kaggle.com/code/hboyang/s6e8-rank-logit-regime-fusion-lb0-97125


That notebook also credits the public OOF libraries it consumes. I do not claim
those base models as original work. My additions were the inference-consistency
fix, the risk-conditional blending layer, repeated validation audits, and the
exact-duplicate post-processing.

I also trained local lookup, XGBoost, LightGBM, and Transformer models using
ideas adapted from Tamerlan Omralinov's Apache-2.0 notebook:



Tamerlan Omralinov, S6E8 | Lookup-Transformer + Insights:
https://www.kaggle.com/code/tamerlanomralinov/s6e8-lookup-transformer-insights-lb-0-97041


These local models were useful for experiments but were not the main source of
the final ensemble's performance.

Validation and inference consistency

The source fusion selected its top-level alpha on two ranked OOF inputs, but its
published test path mixed a raw probability input with a ranked input. Since
ROC AUC depends only on ordering, I applied a strict ordinal rank transform to
both inputs on both train and test.

An uncapped nested five-fold search selected blend weights
[0.725, 0.725, 0.725, 0.725, 0.825]. I used their mean, alpha = 0.745, at
inference. A separate fine-grid scan peaked near 0.74, and the 0.70–0.80 region
was broad rather than a sharp optimum.

This correction moved OOF ROC AUC to 0.970422 and improved every one of 15
validation slices checked.

Risk-conditional blending

A single global alpha assumes that the two meta-models have the same relative
value across the full score distribution. That was not true in OOF.

For each row, I computed a risk coordinate as the mean of the two input ranks.
Rows were assigned to equal-width rank bins, and a separate alpha was selected
inside each bin on OOF predictions. Bins containing only one class fell back to
the global alpha. Finally, I ranked the combined predictions again.

I built three variants with 8, 10, and 12 bins and averaged their final ranks.

Exact-duplicate post-processing

Two test rows had complete feature vectors that appeared exactly once in the
training set. Their matching training labels were unambiguous, so I replaced
those two predictions with the corresponding labels. This affected only test
IDs 735378 and 862871.

What did not work

Several ideas looked better in honest OOF but did not transfer to the hidden
test split:



A seven-variable soft student improved all 15 local validation slices, but
its private score was lower.

A depth-9 pair-target-encoding model raised local OOF further, but also lost
on the final leaderboard.

Residual anti-blends achieved the best local OOF (0.970705) and passed
nested-fold audits, yet produced the weakest hidden-test result among the
late candidates.

Expanding the public pool from 205 to 214 members and training additional
Rank-Gauss stacks added almost no OOF value.

Local Lookup-Transformer, XGBoost, and LightGBM models were useful standalone
baselines but were too weak or too correlated to improve the public fusion.


The main lesson was that tiny OOF improvements were not automatically reliable
under the train/test shift. The conservative rank-aligned risk-bin ensemble was
more robust than the more aggressive residual corrections.

Reproducibility notes



Metric: ROC AUC

OOF protocol: stratified five-fold validation, with repeated slice and
nested-weight audits for promoted blends

Final blend alpha: 0.745 before conditional weighting

Conditional variants: 8, 10, and 12 risk bins

Final post-processing: strict percentile rank plus two exact-duplicate labels


Thanks again to all public contributors. The most useful part of this
competition was learning how much care is needed when transferring a small OOF
gain into a leaderboard ensemble.
