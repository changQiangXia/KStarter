When first approaching this dataset and taking a look at the evaluation metric, it seems pretty clear that we should use regression models to attempt to make our predictions. However, this is not an ordinary regression problem. @siukeitin (congratulations on reaching master btw!) pointed out very early in the competition that despite there being 15289 training samples, there are only 776 unique target values. Another important piece of information for the following trick is that the R² score of a tuned XGBoost model is 0.831 (reported by @sergiosaharovskiy in this notebook). This essentially means that the model is a good fit for the data and we can be fairly confident about the predictions it outputs.

Now for the trick (I am only calling this a trick because it is only really applicable in a competition setting)

First we need to store all of the unique targets in an array:

 = np.unique(train[])


Once we are done making our predictions on the train &amp; test data:

oof_preds = model(X_val)
test_preds += model(test) / n_splits


We can round our predictions to the nearest unique target value:

oof_preds = [(unique_targets,  =  x: (x - ))    oof_preds]
test_preds = [(unique_targets,  =  x: (x - ))    test_preds]


I have personally seen a ~0.3 improvement for MAE in both CV and LB scores using this method. Nothing groundbreaking but as I've experienced in past Kaggle competitions, a small improvement like this could be the difference between placing in the top 10 vs. placing in the top 50.
