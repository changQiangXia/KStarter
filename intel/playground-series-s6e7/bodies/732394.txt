29th Place: FT-Transformer + Exact-Value Target Encoding, and a +643-place case for trusting your CV

TL;DR — Public 0.95034 (rank ~672) → Private 0.95070 (29th / 3,356). No public-LB
chasing: both final submissions were chosen weeks in advance by nested cross-validation, and in
hindsight the CV pick was our best private score out of all 14 submissions. The model that did it
is a single FT-Transformer with fold-local exact-value target encoding — plus a long list of
things that did NOT work, which I think is the more useful half of this post.

The public board had v35 at ~rank 672. The private board put it 29th. The gap between our public
and private ranks was not luck: with fit = 39.8k and unhealthy = 57.7k rows out of 690k,
public-LB movement of ±0.001–0.002 is binomial noise on the minority classes. Masaya Kawamata's
"Trust your CV" thread made that case quantitatively mid-competition, and it was the compass for
our endgame — we stopped submitting entirely two weeks before the deadline once CV said nothing
more was left.

The model (v35)

A clean reproduction of @masayakawamata's FT-Transformer-v2 recipe, rebuilt from the notebook's
code rather than its artifacts (all credit for the architecture to him — his own paired ablation
showed the gain comes from the encoding, +0.0012, not from the transformer itself, +0.00003):



Exact-value target encoding of all 13 columns — every distinct numeric value gets its own
target statistic. The health-risk data is quantized enough that exact values carry signal that
binned or smoothed TEs blur away.

Fitted inside every outer training fold — the TE tables never see the fold they encode.
This is the difference between a real 0.9507 and a leaky 0.96 that collapses on private.

Single ensemble member (n_ens=1): four members cost 4x GPU time for noise-level gains.

Fixed 16-epoch budget, no early stopping, PLR-lite numeric embeddings, prior-correction of the
predicted class distribution.


What did NOT work (32 falsified levers, the honest ledger)

Everything below was implemented, cross-validated with the same nested protocol, and rejected —
posting these so you don't have to rediscover them:



TabPFN v2 as an ensemble leg — error correlation 0.914 with the GBDT legs (we expected
~0.2 from a "different prior"); the stacker assigned it zero weight. Foundation-model
diversity was an illusion here.

Row-segment specialists (e.g. a dedicated model for rows with missing sleep features) —
no segment ever beat the global model on its own segment.

Corrected-KNN density features — public 0.94987, worse than either final.

Greedy hill-climb over all saved legs — converged back to v31's weights; ensembling was
saturated months of levers ago.

Ratio/product feature engineering — duplicated what the exact-value TEs already encode.

Class-multiplier micro-search beyond a simple prior^beta correction — pure CV noise.


The meta-lesson we took from a previous Playground (s6e6, where a public-anchor final cost us
~335 private ranks): the public LB is a small-sample estimate dominated by minority-class noise,
and every point of contact you give it corrupts your selection. This time the discipline paid.

The gap to the top, quantified

2nd place (0.95085, +0.00015 over us — congrats @kava1, great writeup) used the same best base
family (Kawamata's FTT), but blended 18 diverse base predictors with class-specific simplex
weights (an 18×3 weight matrix optimized by SLSQP on smooth OOF LogLoss, then Nelder-Mead class
multipliers for balanced accuracy). His ensemble's OOF (0.950737) beat our v31's (0.950579) by
almost exactly his private margin. The lesson repeats across Playgrounds: past a strong single
model, the remaining edge is breadth of genuinely diverse bases — not deeper ensembling of one
lineage (which is exactly the lever our hill-climb correctly reported as empty).

One practical GPU note

Kaggle still assigns P100s (sm_60), and the preinstalled PyTorch wheel has no sm_60 kernels —
your first CUDA op dies with no kernel image is available. The notebook detects the GPU and
conditionally installs the cu126 wheel on P100 only (never blind-reinstall torch — the
torchvision/numpy cascade is worse than the disease). Pinning the T4 via kernel metadata
("machine_shape": "NvidiaTeslaT4") avoids the issue entirely for API pushes.

Training notebook (full reproduction, runs end-to-end on Kaggle GPU): https://www.kaggle.com/code/satyapb2002/29th-place-ftt-exact-value-target-encoding
