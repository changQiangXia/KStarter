Solution: One CatBoost Is All You Need (CatBoost model with no feature engineering)
Code: https://www.kaggle.com/code/rohanrao/automl-grand-prix-1st-place-solution

Setup

Due to the large size of the data, we first built baseline models on smaller samples for quicker iterations and finally moved to training on the whole data during the latter stages. Using more data always led to better results so we ended up training our final models with stratified k-fold CV.

Features

We tried a whole bunch of features across models but nothing worked for us. So our final solution has no feature engineering at all. Just tuning the model on the raw features (with some raw interactions thrown in) gave sufficiently stable and high scores.

Models

CatBoost was a clear winner outperforming all other models we tried, including LightGBM, XGBoost, LinearModels and various AutoMLs, so we spent the majority of our time in tuning the hyperparameters of CatBoost. Running it with GPU on the entire data was better than running it on a smaller sample with CPU.

Submission

Our final submission was just one CatBoost model averaged across folds which was good enough for 1st place. The model had a CV of 0.895x and LB of 0.896x (might vary mildly based on package version, seed and accelerator).

Code

The entire code is published in the notebook [AutoML Grand Prix] 1st Place Solution.

Due to the runtime limitation on Kaggle Notebooks, the above notebook runs only 5-fold achieving a CV of 0.89505 and LB of 0.89586. Increasing that to more folds (like 10-fold or 15-fold) will give little better scores which we ran on local machines for our submission.
