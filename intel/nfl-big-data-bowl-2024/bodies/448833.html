Tackling or Blocking? You can’t have one without the other.

What do you think is more important: blocking or tackling in football?

By Rick Love

"As an offensive lineman supporter, the blocking execution, both as a unit and individual technique is so important, but deception, speed, and individual athleticism play into it as well. Nothing is more rewarding to me than a perfectly blocked play leaving a gaping hole for our skilled backs to run through or a pocket of blockers protecting the quarterback as he releases a long td pass."

"On the other hand, as defensive coordinator, his job was to get each defender to fulfill his assignment on each play so that they were in position to make a tackle. Once there, we focused on correct tackling techniques, starting with a standing fit in against a standing ball carrier, advancing to a short walk into that fit position, and following with different degrees of live contact."

"In short, blocking skills are a very important part of the offense’s success, as tackling is a very important part of defensive success. But the other things I pointed to, technique, pursuit, conditioning, deception and misdirection, are very important parts of the team and individual offensive and defensive effort. it’s kind of like the old song “Love and Marriage”. You can’t have one without the other."

Total, Solo, and Assisted Tackles

1 solo and 1 assisted tackle = 2 total tackles (1 solo + 1 assist = 2 total tackles). Or

1 solo and 1 assisted tackle = 1.5 total tackles

A solo tackle is a tackle by just that one player.

An assist is one where the player makes a tackle along with at least one other player at the same time (i.e. two or more players tackle the ball carrier at the same time).

Total is the solo tackle plus assists.

OT (Offensive Tackle) generally the tallest and heaviest position in football

"A tackle is a playing position in American football. Historically, in the one-platoon system prevalent in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, a tackle played on both offense and defense. In the modern system of specialized units, offensive tackle and defensive tackle are separate positions, and the stand-alone term "tackle" refers to the offensive tackle position only."

The offensive tackle (OT,T)

"The offensive tackle (OT, T) is a position on the offensive line, left and right. Like other offensive linemen, their job is to block: to physically keep defenders away from the offensive player who has the football and enable him to advance the football and eventually score a touchdown. The term "tackle" is a vestige of an earlier era of football in which the same players played both offense and defense."

"A tackle is the strong position on the offensive line. They power their blocks with quick steps and maneuverability. The tackles are mostly in charge of the outside protection. Usually they defend against defensive ends, but they do also have to defend against defensive tackles, especially if the corresponding guard on their side pulls. In the NFL, offensive tackles often measure over 6 ft 4 in (193 cm) and 300 lb (140 kg). Offensive tackle generally the tallest and heaviest position in football."
