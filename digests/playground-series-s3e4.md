### 30th Place Solution 

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/382447

This comp was an interesting one, and for me the hardest of the Playground Series this month. The addition of the time component to the data, combined with how the train and test sets were split by time, made me more unsure of how to best build a local CV schema and unsure of its reliability. In the end, I used sklearn's TimeSeriesSplit for CV, and at the very least it seemed fairly reliable for optimizing hyperparameters.

My final solution was pretty simple, just an average of 50 XGBoost models with the same hyperparameters, trained using Stratified KFold with 5 folds and 10 repeats. I ended up using the entire train dataset plus the instances of fraud from the original Credit Card Fraud data.

There were 2 things that I think helped my score a little. First, I added transformed versions of the V features created by subtracting the daily average for that feature. This was inspired by @paddykb's comment about how many features behaved differently between day 1 and day 2, and upon further observation, it seemed to that the general up-down trend of the features throughout a day was similar between day 1 and day 2, and the main difference was that this trend was shifted up or down between days. So I figured that subtracting out the daily average might help uncover some more signal. This did not improve my Public LB score, but it gave me a boost of +.0015 on the Private LB score.

The second addition was inspired by @siukeitin's comment about using the V features to identify customers. Out of curiosity, I ran a check to see if grouping the train data by any 2 V features would result in pure groups of all fraud or all non-fraud, and I found that the combination of V14 and V21 did just that. This gave me 466 rows in the test set that had a (V14, V21) that also existed in the train set, and based on the train data, I predicted 0 for those rows. This boosted both my Public and Private LB scores by +.0004.

Sadly, it was my decision to include the instances of fraud from the original data that prevented me from placing higher, and I suspect that anyone who scored .83 or above probably did it without any original data. Had I excluded the original data, I would've scored .8333, enough for 1st place. With all of the well-documented differences between the original and synthetic data this time around, my instinct was telling me to not use the original data, but it was hard to go against what my CV and the Public LB were telling me. A good lesson for the next one.

### Use your Time wisely

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/380771

Before jumping a throw the usual stuff, let's first identify something:

Train dataset has Time from 0 to 33.5 hours after the first transactions. Test on the other side goes from 33.5 to 48 hours. We have non overlapping time windows

Features can be related to time, if we assume all transactions are in the same time zone. This can be easily verified by plotting any feature against the time. Do you spot any pattern?

Intuition may suggest that frauds happens at certain times during the day, we don't have clear picture of the exact hour, but the pattern exists.

Implement a cross-validation strategy where the validation data is 14.5 hours ahead of the training data and carefully analyze the performance of the model on the fold where the test hours are identical to the validation hours.

### Don't underestimate solo models - 54th place

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e4/discussion/382443

Another fun installment of the Playground Series has concluded!

The data was very noisy and the target variable was highly unbalanced (roughly 99.8% negative, 0.02% positive). For my solution I used @samir95's cross validation strategy which seemed to work very well.

Adversarial validation notebooks by @bcruise, @craigmthomas, and @kaggleqrdl helped to sniff out the large differences between the train, test, and original datasets. Because of the large difference between each of the datasets, it was a difficult decision whether to add the original or a subset of the original data to the training data. I did end up using the original data but only a small subset original.query("Class == 1 and Time > 120580") which was only 132 rows. For the reasoning behind Time > 120580 let's take a look at this plot by @satoshiss:

[图 1: inbox%2F10590800%2F983c80d85bbf8a742d2f56ab2c2ff54a%2Ftransaction-time.png]

Blue is the training data and red is the test data. The training data provided contained no entries with Time > 120580 so I believed adding this would help the model to generalize better to the test set.

My final best scoring submission ended up being a single Catboost model with no hyper parameter tuning (notebook here). I would really like to hear about the model(s) that you used and their performance. That's all from me for now, see you in the next episode!
