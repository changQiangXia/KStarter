### [1st] Disbelief

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-mar-2022/discussion/316271

I was quite surprised to find that the LB shakeup was as high as it was. I almost didn't check tonight because I was so disappointed in my scores. While I would like to congratulate myself as being some sort of data-science genius, I think I got more than a little bit lucky in this case. I know many smarter, more experienced competitors worked much harder than me this month. Sometimes you win the lottery, I guess. 

I am grateful to all the kagglers who have shared their insight via the discussions and code. Specifically, I was helped by @Pack-Man's AutoML notebook and @Martynov Andrey's Hybrid Regressors notebook. From the first I took a way to likelihood encode based on the cross between hours-minute time and location. From the second, I liked the post-processing. 

Broad Overview

The winning score was a single lgbm with no post-processing. I kinda ran out of time. First, I created a substantial amount of likelihood encodings and lag features. Unlike most public kernels, I used many other features than the medians. Second, I found the best subset of the features using Optuna. 

For validation, I used the data that were exactly 1 week before the test. I filtered the train data to only be during the same day and hour-minute combinations as the test period. The feature creation of the winning model is here and the feature selection of the winning model is found here. 

The Lags and Likelihood Encodings

This notebook is the code I used to create both the lag features and the encodings. For the lagged features, I found means, variances, medians, minimums, maximums, and 1 interval shifts. I took this for every x-y-direction combination on both the day and weekday. I used both 3,5, and 10 day rolling windows and expanding windows. 

The likelihood encodings took the minimus, maximums, medians, variances, and means for every xy and x-y-direction combination at all hour-minute combinations. 

The whole process takes around 5-10 minutes to run.

Optuna for Feature Selection and Train Filtering

After step 1 I had too many features. Given the surplus of features, I used Optuna to pick the best features. Specifically, I used trial.suggest_categorical(feat_in_question, [True, False]), for each of the features I considered. After 300 trials, Optuna got very good at finding the best features. Moreover, I saw that many notebooks only training on weekday 0. I had optuna find whether I should train on only day 0 and, additionally, if I should only train on the same hour-minute times as the test. After the Optuna trials, I just picked the best features and submitted the notebook. The code for this is here.

Failures

Wide Neural Networks. Given the spatial relationship of the data, it would make sense to train a regressor on all the spatial points at a given time simultaneously. This did not work out for me, however. 

Special Thanks

I want to give a special thanks to the "How to Win a Data Science Competition" course on coursera, put on by the Higher School of Economics. This course taught me the basics of likelihood encodings, which account for much of the score here. The encodings I did here were not very pretty as they did not use k-fold validation to prevent leakage. Another case of not having enough time. 

I have to thank the general kaggle community for all their help along my journey as a data scientist. From dropping out of school two years ago to working as a data scientist now, kagglers have helped me learn much more than I ever could on my own. Thank you, reader, for being part of this great community. Keep kaggling.

It is 11:30 here, and I wake up at 4:45 to go to work. Thanks for reading!

### As simple as possible, but not simpler...

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-mar-2022/discussion/310642

Yesterday I spent quite some time trying to get useful predictions with a random forest (and with ExtraTrees and with HuberRegressor). 

I wasted my time: Today I realized that simple predictions without machine learning are much better than all my random forest attempts. It gives little consolation that a hundred other people wasted their time as well ☹️

The method is easily explained: We have to predict traffic congestion for one Monday afternoon. The train dataset contains the congestion values for two dozen Monday afternoons. If we compute the median congestion for every time and place and submit these medians, we get an lb score of 4.967 (notebook is here).

Why does this method work? Because the afternoon of September 30 is not much different from other Monday afternoons.

Of course this simple method neglects that not all Mondays are equal. Traffic congestion may be affected by weather, construction sites, events and so on. To take this into account, I'm going to try to use the morning traffic to predict the afternoon traffic. It is plausible that if morning traffic deviates from average, afternoon traffic will deviate from average, too.

My mistake was that I simply followed the format of the data: The dataset comes as a table with rows, and I tried to predict the congestion value of every row from the features of the same row. But this is not the character of time series. The specialty of time series is that every row contains a timestamp and that you have to use the data of several rows to predict the target of a future row. Using the morning rows to predict the afternoon rows is just an application of this principle.

### [Apache Spark][Scala]My second tabular competition, impressions and my solution

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-mar-2022/discussion/313882

Hello, 

like I said in the title this is my second tabular competition and I must say I am enjoying this one much more than the previous one.

Competitions are for me the most engaging part of the Kaggle experience, there are many good parts like the data sets and the great community, but competitions are the best for me.

I implemented the solution for this challenge using Apache Spark ML and Scala as the programming language.

For the implementation I extracted the day of the week and the hour as separated columns from the date column, and use these two fields along side with the direction and X and Y fields.

I used One Hot Encoders for the indexed string fields, a Random Forest regressor and a cross validator. I split my test data in order to save about 10% for evaluation and I did some hyper parameter tuning.

Doing this I was able to get the score of 5.320 which currently sets me at the 66% percentile, not great by any means but decent in my opinion :)
