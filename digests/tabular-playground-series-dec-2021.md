### 2# place winning solution.

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-dec-2021/discussion/298304

Preface:

I was extremely excited January 1 morning when I awoke and saw that I came in 2nd in the competition. 

My main goal for this competition was to create a great kernel to support further competition development and encourage other competitors. The kernel did well… I decided to switch gears and work on the Leaderboard. I set a plan, prepared a design document and defined very first experiments.

Data Science Approach:

I started to collect as much information as I could from the discussion and kernels.

What I decided for myself first is that I would work on the original data set link closely. I worked to do as much as possible on the features comparison, selection and elimination. 

At the same time @chryzal posted his notebook link on the features engineering. Before that I had already applied Euclidian distance for my model. 

1. Feature engineering: it seemed that feature engineering was justified, but I could not agree with @chryzal on aggregated features. My runs on bringing the competition dataset as closer to the original dataset scored extremely well on CV but failed on the test data. Extra feature clipping, applying the soil masks (p.s. did you know that some Trees (aka Cover_Types) simply didn't grow at the specific soils in the original dataset?) failed. Tens of experiments failed. Only Euclidian distance, Manhattan distance and Aspect fix worked. I ran out of ideas. I was about to quit.

2. Model and Second Breath: I tried different models architectures and got convinced that almost all of my models converged identically. My best performer ended up being link. I did not believe in the SeLU from the very beginning. I stacked my 10 best performers (a.k.a soft voting) and it was a breakthrough. (p.s. col_drop=['Id', 'Soil_Type7', 'Soil_Type15']). Then, another round of experiments and another breakthrough. Same architecture but different batch_sizes -> soft voting of the best performers. 

3. Demotivation 1.0: I woke up another morning and I saw 30 people on top of me copied the same notebook. It seemed to me that the competition was becoming a circus or a baby playground (I have not still figured out the right analogy).

4. Pseudolabling: It is a big "no-no" for Data Scientist (at least for me) to merge train and test data. I was extremely sceptical to it. But since I could not be certain in anything at that moment already, I gave it a try but with my models. I quickly realized that my models performance only deteriorates from it. Since it worked for someone, but proved not working for me, I decided to stay away from it.

5. Demotivation 2.0: I woke up another morning and I saw another tens of people ahead of me with score 0.9709->0.9711 copying the same blend based on other people works (that time my score was 0.9707).

6. Final blend or if you cannot fight it, improve it: I could not fight it any longer improving my models endlessly without external help. It was obvious that others just copied already proven results. I looked closely at 0.9709->0.9711 opened blends. I removed anything related to pseudo labels. I stacked 3 models of mine (score 0.9707) and other people works.

 Finally, I got: 

3 models of 0.9707 (my best performer)

1 model @mlanhenke tps-12-g-res-variable-selection-nn-keras

3 models @kaaveland TPS202112 - Reasonable XGBoost model

2 models @ambrosm TPSDEC21-12 Eliminate cover type 4!

I iterated over tens of experiments a small version of it can be found at. Tree of experiment small version.

I hope I have not forgotten to mention/reference/upvote anybody's work or contribution to this achievement. Thanks for reading. It was a long post.

### TPS competitions - my lessons learned in 2021

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-dec-2021/discussion/296842

I remember the day I joined Kaggle … I didn't know what I was doing …. Today … is a day of summary of my TPS 2021 competitions … what I have learnt 

Practice, practice, practice. Practicing make you perfect. Minimize talking about AI … maximize practice. My experience grows with the number of lines of code written (it's thousands, thousands … lines and hundreds of notebooks - I remember one competition where I generated over 60 different notebooks).

Data, data … data … 90% … 9% model, 1% tuning - before you move on you must know everything about dataset. It is a broad topic … it has many elements … but it is the basis.

The solutions are usually simpler than we think.

I like to be surprised by clever solutions proposed by smarter than myself … it's such a nice feeling when someone breaks your head … oh, there are many of them on Kaggle … respect!

I love the moments of Kaggle breakthrough … when the whole world stands still for a few days, no one improves the result, then someone shares an insight, a solution and the whole world moves forward … it shows what power lies in cooperation and sharing. It is such behavior that builds progress and innovation. Breakthroughs like this are often experienced at Kaggle competitions … valuable experience.

Usually the best answer is "I don't know" … we have to check it and conduct many experiments to prove the thesis. If someone, without conducting an experiment, says that "it's like this" then 98% is wrong.

I like the moment when I make a submission on Kaggle and … wait for the results. I learned not to build expectations … so often I greeted the "goose" to see later that the solution is much worse than I thought ….

Stay up to date with SoTA … and ML / AI news …

Learn the basics - master Python … numpy, pandas. Then Keras / Tensorflow or / and Pytorch … classification (implement some basic architectures from scratch), then add a head to it and make a simple detector …. and create, experiment and … have fun!

Talk to people … with enthusiasts … on Kaggle I met people from all over the world … France, Germany, Japan, China, Korea, USA, Turkey …. Poland … we are still trying to figure out how to "push" the limits ", how to jump over another 0.001 in the result … Sometimes it is just one hyperparameter… other times hundreds lines of code …

look for a mentor …. someone from whom you will be able to learn how to make a good solution even better …. 

if you want to develop, you MUST share … your knowledge and solutions …

is it possible to be in TOP100 kagglers in one year … yes it is … :) 

I appereciate all of you who help me grow here. Thank you!

Updated 27.12.2021

I often see EDA …. without "so what" …. just … good looking charts … absolutely oudstanding charts …. but … there is no conclusion … "so what" … 

Updated 30.12.2021

Why is my notebook rated so low? Why does it only have 20 votes? Check the number of forks. You will discover its true value and usefulness. Sometimes my notebook only has 20-30 votes or it lacks a few points to gold … I look at the number of forks and I can't believe …

Stay focused …. I know that daily there is a lot new ideas in competition (or your project) … stay focused … “If you chase two rabbits…You will not catch either one.” Luckily Kaggle provide only 5 submission daily … this limitation is for thinking … not Kaggle's malice.

### Fix these features to get a significant boost in accuracy!!

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-dec-2021/discussion/293373

Following are the four features I fixed which gave me a massive boost in score:

Aspect

Hillshade_9am

Hillshade_Noon

Hillshade_3pm

Aspect is the compass direction that a terrain faces. Here it is expressed in Sexagesimal system where the angle lies in the range (0, 359) degrees. In this feature, however, there are some values which are less than 0 and some values which are greater than 359. It will be better If we fix those values so that It lies in the given range. This is fairly easy to do in this case because upon a closer inspection you will find that all the values in this column lies in the range (-360, 720). So, adding 360 to angles smaller than 0 and subtracting 360 from angles greater than 359 will do the work. This is how I have done it:

train_df["Aspect"][train_df["Aspect"] < 0] += 360
train_df["Aspect"][train_df["Aspect"] > 359] -= 360

test_df["Aspect"][test_df["Aspect"] < 0] += 360
test_df["Aspect"][test_df["Aspect"] > 359] -= 360

The next three features are the Hillshade features. Hillshade, basically, is a 3D representation of a surface. Hillshade is created by measuring luminosity of certain patches of a terrain that results when a source of light is casted at a particular angle. It's a shade of grey so all the values must lie in the range (0, 255) which is also what the data description in the original competition says. However, In both train and test datasets, there are certain rows with hillshade value more than 255 or less than 0. This may be the result of recording error. It seems that the negative values refer to the darkest shade, which has the value of 0, and the values greater then 255 refer to brightest shade, which has the value of 255 and, hence, It would be better to replace all the negative values with 0 and values greater than 255 with 255. Here is how I have done it:

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_9am"] < 0, "Hillshade_9am"] = 0
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_9am"] < 0, "Hillshade_9am"] = 0

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_Noon"] < 0, "Hillshade_Noon"] = 0
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_Noon"] < 0, "Hillshade_Noon"] = 0

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_3pm"] < 0, "Hillshade_3pm"] = 0
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_3pm"] < 0, "Hillshade_3pm"] = 0

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_9am"] > 255, "Hillshade_9am"] = 255
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_9am"] > 255, "Hillshade_9am"] = 255

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_Noon"] > 255, "Hillshade_Noon"] = 255
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_Noon"] > 255, "Hillshade_Noon"] = 255

train_df.loc[train_df["Hillshade_3pm"] > 255, "Hillshade_3pm"] = 255
test_df.loc[test_df["Hillshade_3pm"] > 255, "Hillshade_3pm"] = 255

After doing these corrections, my leaderboard score improved from 0.95631 to 0.95656 while using the same NN architecture.

### Focal loss, a solution at the loss level for imbalanced data

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/tabular-playground-series-dec-2021/discussion/293072

In the recent years, when I've dealt with classification problems with unbalanced data and I could use neural networks and gradient boosting solutions, I've found more useful to change the loss function than to retouch the data (I would have to justify it) or apply weights modifying the existing loss.

The focal loss is a loss that has been devised for object detection problems where the background is more prominent than the objects to be detected. You can find the original paper here: https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02002

[图 1: focal_loss.png]

As you increase the gamma value, you put more emphasis on hard to classify examples. There is clearly a trade-off for this (high gamma values can be detrimental), but overall if you set the right value it should perform much better than using other tricks for imbalanced data.

You can learn more about focal loss by reading these three articles:

https://paperswithcode.com/method/focal-loss

https://amaarora.github.io/2020/06/29/FocalLoss.html

https://medium.com/swlh/focal-loss-what-why-and-how-df6735f26616

Focal loss has been though for binary problems in objected detection, but it can be used also for multiclass problems (with slight modification) and for tabular data and other general classification problems.

For TensorFlow you can find a package implementing both the binary and the categorical focal loss function here: https://github.com/artemmavrin/focal-loss/blob/master/docs/source/index.rst
