### 4th place solution: Stacking with XGB + Pseudo labeling + metric optimizing.

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e2/discussion/480939

Thanks again for an interesting series challenge, always a fun and good area for testing and experiment new models and ideas, benchmark against the old ones!

Big shakeup in this competition, personally I moved 255 places. But this is expected with a 20/80 ratio in the test set, and one should heavily weight the best local cross-validation in such scenario.

In this challenge we had a multi-class problem, we had similar not so long ago in the Multi-Class Prediction of Cirrhosis Outcomes so I picked up some ideas from there from my 4th place solution. 

https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e26/discussion/464863

Solution

A stacking approach with XGB as meta learning and different SOTA solutions as extra added stacking features from each prediction. Final inference includes optimized accuracy metric and 9 predictions of different version of the stacking code and other diverse solutions for a count of max class per row.

### 2nd solution write-up

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s4e2/discussion/481062

This is the first time my solution falls in top positions. 

Here are my general observations:

Normally, CV performance is positively correlated with the LB performance. However, at some point, CV performance gets improved, but the LB performance just goes down, which is the point of overfitting. At this point, to improve the LB performance, ensembling is necessary.

The dataset for this competition is small, hence large shake-ups are expected similar to other playground competitions. My best notebook does not have very high public LB score, but a high private LB score.

Here are what worked and didn't work in terms of CV, public LB and private LB. (Note that for notebooks with different datasets, we cannot compare their CV performances).

1. What worked:

Data

Adding original dataset: As usual, adding original dataset to the generated train dataset improves the performance. My best solution actually adds the original dataset four times.

CV folds: I did HPO for 5 folds, and use these hyper-params for the final notebook with 20 folds. 

Model

Basic feature encoding with standard scaler (z score) and one-hot encoding

Only two models, i.e. xgb and lgbm, are used in my best notebook. HPO is done with optuna on this basic feature encoding.

Probability thresholding: Kudo to this notebook from @KULDEEP RATHORE . I applied this trick with CV and used the averaged weights.

2. What didn't work:

Data

Pseudo-label: For this competition, it didn't bring a better LB performance for me.

Model

AutoML: I tried autogluon, flaml, auto-sklearn. Flaml gave me decent performance (both public and private) and it's the best of three, but still lower than the simple ensembling of xgb and lgbm. In the last competition, autogluon performs very well, but it is not the case for this competition, perhaps because of the small volume of data. Auto-sklearn gave the lowest performance of three.

Feature engineering: For this competition, feature engineering didn't work at all, even with a simple BMI feature. With extra engineered features, I could get better CV performance, but when I submitted the solution, the public LB performance was always worse. Eventually, I ended up using basic feature encoding only.

Stacking: Stacking ensembling (sklearn) didn't bring an improvement for this competition.

Weighted ensemble: Tried weighted ensemble with optuna for xgb, lgbm, hgb, gb, rf, etc using their probability outputs. The CV performance was improved, but the pubic LB performance was worse.
