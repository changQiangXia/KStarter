### Useful additional data sources for your story!

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggle-survey-2022/discussion/359047

This year's edition of the competition for the best notebook describing the data from this year's Kaggle survey has started, so probably many people are looking for an idea and inspiration for the subject of their notebook. As you can use additional data sources, I I wrote down some interesting suggestions in my opinion that can enrich our analysis if they fit the topic well. I invite you to a short overview!

1. Kaggle surveys from past years: 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2017

The results from previous years' surveys fit well with all narratives related to time. If we want to see how the aspects described in the survey have changed over time, it is worth using the old data. The format and type of question has been heavily standardized since 2018, only data from 2017 require a large amount of data cleansing and recoding.

2. Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2011-2022

Every year for 11 years, a similar type of survey appears on StackOverflow, the largest forum for people using programming languages and more. The use of this data can be used to mention Python coders information in a Kaggle survey or be a starting point for comparing data workers to other IT parts.

3. World Development Indicators or others datasets about countries

If we want to do analyzes where the country of residence of the respondents is an important aspect, it may be a good idea to use other information about these countries (e.g. demographic, economic or social) to add additional context to our analyzes. There are a lot of such databases on Kaggle, just enter the keyword "countries" in the datasets tab and read the various databases and find out whether the information contained in them can be combined in an interesting narrative related to the Kaggle survey.

4. Meta Kaggle

Data on Kaggle itself has been used frequently in previous editions, especially when the notebook's narrative is focused on the Kaggle portal itself and how it works. In this database we will find gigabytes of data about competitions, users, databases, notebooks and discussions.

5. Data Science Job Salaries

If the narrative covers the financial aspect that is part of the survey, we also have other sources talking about this topic in the industry. Many portals publish data sicence job offers around the world with an indicative salary, which may bring interesting conclusions in conjunction with data from this competition.

There is a lot of data that can be used - it all depends on the subject of the notebook and its needs. If you have any additional suggestions, feel free to share in the comment :) 

Good luck in the competition!

### 1st place solution approach "behind the scenes" - part II: idea

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggle-survey-2022/discussion/374969

Hi, 

Today I will tell you a bit about the idea for my notebook, where it came from and why in this form.

I have participated in this competition 2 times before and was not completely satisfied with my analysis. When we look at the data from this competition, or more broadly from the majority of surveys on the market, we can easily come to the conclusion that the result database contains columns in a qualitative form (or in a dummy form if it is a multi-choise or multi-category, e.g. in the case of a country, age, etc.). 

If we are well versed in descriptive statistics, we know that variables in a qualitative form are generally less interesting to analyze, because the number of metrics or ways to analyze them is much more limited than in the case of quantitative variables on a continuous scale.

But we do not have to rely on competition data in an unchanged form, if there is no quantitative data, then why not come to it … after a few transformations?

And so the idea to create "factors" was born. The factor works on the principle that we specify a grouping variable (in this case, country) to which we add a factor condition (e.g. the percentage of people under 30). In this way, using the classic "group by" and "summarise", it will obtain a table in which the rows contain different levels of the grouping variable (in my case, countries) and the columns contain the desired continuous variable whose values are in the range from 0 to 1. On such data, we can already use a whole range of methods that would be impossible without transformation (e.g. distribution analysis, regression, correlation using the perason coefficient, scatterplot… you can go on and on).

I focused on a comparative analysis between countries for two reasons: 

Firstly, I was very curious about data science/kaggling in other countries 😃

Secondly, taking part in previous editions and looking through the work of other people, it is easy to see that the country of residence of the respondent really says a lot about him in every sphere (demographic, educational, technological professional), so I expected a lot of differences and interesting conclusions even before the data transformation.

About factories, I came up with 15 different ones, looking through all the columns and choosing the most interesting ones in my opinion. What I wanted was for the columns to be different from each other and describe different spheres (demography, education/science, technology and professional work) to have a holistic view of the respondents from these countries.

This is an example of how it gives a unique character to the analysis that is not repeated later in other notebooks and at the same time greatly extends the analysis. The conclusion is simple: if the data in its pure form seems insufficient to us, it is worth thinking about transformations (filtering, grouping, aggregations) that will make the same input data more interesting.

### 1st place solution approach "behind the scenes" - part I: before development

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/kaggle-survey-2022/discussion/374157

Hello everyone,

As promised, I wanted to share with you how I worked on my submission in this year's competition.

However, this will not be a post only about the methods and the code itself, but more about how I approached the problem and how the work on the competition looked like. On Kaggle you can easily find similar material from model contest winners about how they approach the problem and I found it inspiring so I thought I'd share my thoughts on the best notebook analytics competitions which is a completely different issue.

The post will be divided into several parts, which I will successively upload in next days/weeks

First of all… unfortunately, there are no shortcuts when it comes to time. The development of the notebook itself took about 80 hours of development work, but the the whole process starts with an idea and design.

Therefore, I just started walking and thinking about what to create this year and in what form. It is time consuming because it is a decision that I was not able to make quickly and I wanted to think it over very well, therefore I would write down various ideas and put them aside for a 2/3 days and then come back to them with a fresh mind and I was better able to determine whether is this idea suitable for a contest or is it better to think of something else.

The development of the notebook actually starts when we take the data, but before that I started making little sketches of charts to verify that my idea made sense. However, the project itself required a lot of changes, but decent sketches allowed me to think about many things before development. Thanks to this, when I start development, I already have an idea, a general project and a clear direction in which the development should go, which means that I will not have to go back to the basics.

In practice, then also a lot of things changed according to the original plan, but this approach still saved a lot of work and brain resources for the intense development stage. I'm sure if I hadn't thought the idea through before starting development, the notebook wouldn't have been so good.

A common approach that people take on such contests is to start working right away with loading data and try everything step by step in a spontaneous way. It is not a good direction in my opinion, the notebook then often does not have a clear narrative, a unique idea and elements, and consistency between subsequent parts that can be thought out in advance. The plan does not have to be as detailed as possible, and the design does not have to be long, the mere existence of these two elements, even in a small form, makes the work much easier in my opinion.
