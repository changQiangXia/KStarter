### Music Information Retrieval. Tempo Estimation (ANNs, LSTM). DJing: Genre Blend & BPM ranges.

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s5e9/discussion/603307

Music Tempo Estimation via Neural Networks – A Comparative Analysis

Authors: Mila Soares de Oliveira de Souza, Pedro Nuno de Souza Moura, Jean-Pierre Briot

"Music Information Retrieval (MIR) is the field of study that aims to extract, analyze and provide information from a song. Basic methodologies for Music Information Retrieval can include audio signal processing, musical perception, among others. Some examples of MIR research involve tempo estimation, identifying musical styles and comparison of similarity between two songs."

"Estimating the tempo of a song is considered one of the most fundamental tasks of MIR. 

The tempo of a musical piece corresponds to the number of beats that would be counted in one minute (beats per minute, or bpm). In general terms, it can be understood as the speed at which humans often tap their fingers or feet as they listen to music. When listening to a song, it is often generalized that music sounding “fast” to a listener should have a longer tempo than songs that 

seem comparatively slower. "

"Rhythm is one of the most important characteristics of music, and it can be said that it is one of the factors that makes certain musical genres easily recognizable (e.g.: waltz in 3/4 and rock in 4/4-time signatures). Some of them more often work with faster tempo – like heavy metal – while others often work with comparatively shorter tempo – like reggae. Identifying such tempo could have interesting applications in other contexts, such as, for example, a recommendation system for similar songs, which would suggest tracks considering similar tempi and/or other characteristics."

"As for tempo estimation using artificial neural networks (ANNs), the first evidences were found at late 2000s-early 2010s. Böck uses a bidirectional Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) in order to map beat activations, after which the results are further processed with comb filters. The work of Carayannis also uses a convolutional neural network to derive a beat activation function, then identify the tempo in later steps. A neural network model considered state of the art (for tempo estimation) was proposed and further refined in 2015. The model is a bidirectional LSTM, aided by external modules which process detected beat activations." 

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.09208

### Are 50% of Kaggle Playground Regression Competitions created from Random Targets?

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s5e9/discussion/604028

Each Kaggle Playground competition is synthetic data generated from an original dataset. Is it the case that many Kaggle Playground competition original datasets are just random numbers with no signal?

We can evaluate original datasets with an XGB model. We can train an XGB on the original data features and target. Then we randomly shuffle the target and train 100 more XGB models. Lastly, we compare the CV RMSE scores of the XGB trained with original target versus the XGB trained with the random target.

If the CV score from using the original target is within z-score -2 to 2 of the CV score from using random targets, we conclude that the original data is just random numbers. In the past 6 regression competitions, 50% or 3 out of 6 were just random numbers :-(

### 26th Place: FE, Pseudo-Labels, Residuals

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s5e9/discussion/610264

I felt this competition was almost like gambling, but I still learned a lot during the process. I spent a few days experimenting, and my final submission ended up as a blend of top public solutions and my own feature engineering and modeling.
1. Feature Engineering & Selection

Started with raw dataset, performed standard cleaning.

Selected 54 best features using a combination of:

Feature importance from XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost.

Permutation importance.

SHAP values.
2.Base Models

I trained 18 different models ❤️thanks to Mikhail Naumov, your notebook was a huge inspiration, including:

6 XGBoost with tuned hyperparameters (Optuna).

4 LightGBM.

3 HistGradientBoostingRegressor.

2 YDFRegressor (GradientBoostedTreesLearner).

Ridge and ElasticNet.

Neural Network.

3. Pseudo-Labeling
I generated pseudo-labels from the test set:
Calculated residual errors from multiple models.
Selected the lowest-error samples:

thr_mean = np.percentile(abs(residuals_mean), 30)
thr_std = np.percentile(residuals_std, 30)

Chose around 92,804 pseudo-labels (≈30% of test data).
Retrained my models with these pseudo-labels added to training data.
4. Residual Modeling
After the first round of training, I trained residual models (stacking) to improve predictions and reduce bias.

5. Ensembling Strategy
Final submission was a geometric-like blend of three top public notebooks), plus my own trained model:
Final Prediction
$$
\hat{y} \;=\; 0.5 \cdot \sqrt[3]{\text{sub1} \times \text{sub2} \times \text{sub3}} \;+\; 0.5 \cdot y_{\text{mySub}}
$$

This geometric blend helped stabilize the predictions and slightly improve LB score.
