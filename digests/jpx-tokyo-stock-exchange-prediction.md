### Guessing The Future Winning Solutions!

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jpx-tokyo-stock-exchange-prediction/discussion/324051

'

Alright, Guys! 

I went to read some posts from previous kaggle competitions and came back sharing some of my guesses for what the winning solutions might look like for this competition

(Just for fun and for those who have been asking about this)

First off, this is not the supposed "Winning solution guide" as it might not win. This is just my guess of what the winning solution is supposed to work (Similar to this one).

So, If my hypothesis is wrong, please do not mind calling me stupid!

 
Note: This post is heavily inspired by a post on the G-Research competition about crypto trading but most parts also apply here.

General Framework

Setup

As with most winning solutions, the entire pipeline is divided into 4 major parts:

Preprocessing, especially video frames

Model Architecture, the network implementation and optimization

Ensemble, parts of the technique

TTA augmentations and pseudo-labelling

First, It is probably safe to say that the top solution will refuse to use any public prediction (submission file) or notebook as is. not any "Pretrained Model" for their ensemble. This is mostly the case in every competition: Winning solutions train their pipeline end2end on their own [even if parts of it are derived from public sharings].

Hierarchical Time-series (HTS) 

This is a time-series competition. 

moreover - this can be modeled with hierarchical time series (HTS) model.

This basically means that you have a "model per asset" and "one model for all" maybe even "model per weekday"… and so on. by the end you ensemble and merge all the models.

This method had proven to work over and over and over again in previous competitions.

Most notably the last M5 prediction. the winning solution was an extremely simple LGB running in an HTS fashion.. At the end of the day, It destroyed much more complex solutions with ease. by the way, there were nearly no fancy deep-learning models whatsoever at the top.

Note: It is important to note however that in both recent financial competitions there was no HTS solution at the top.

Future Issues that future notebooks are going to address

Memory limits 

1.0 It might look like the data is small and it won't be a problem. trust me, when running HTS. it will become a problem. You will have to address this head-on.

1.1 You have to find a way to have more and more meaningful features (splits by "type"/date limits)

1.2 lags, aggs, nan cutoffs, etc.. all need to be taken into consideration.

Models

2.1. There is no "winner" here but if there was, I think that the winner of this competition will be an ensemble of NNs [Important will be FC, Tabnet, AutoEncoders..]. They are performing pretty well on the extrapolation of time series data. 

2.2 The simple models will include LGB, XGB, Catboost, HistGBM. And strong solutions will have both.

2.3 Some novel ideas might include ARIMA, SARIMAX with some unique tricks for ensembling.

3.4 Ensembling and stacking, it works.

3.5 About deep learning: I don't think there will be an end2end deep learning solution (like pure LSTM or WaveNet) at the beginning. These are absolutely extremely hard to get right. however, from experience: when you do manage to get them to work (mostly wavenet), they are REALLY GOOD. However, we might see pretty early on some NBEATS or some AutoML NBEATS solution (like gluonTS). these are not as hard to train and they are pretty stable.

3.6 Neural networks that we will see for sure: simple FC networks trained on simple tabular feature engineering (lags, aggs.. etc..). They will also work pretty well on the LB.

Advanced Ideas we will probably see sometime

4.0 Fancy Loss function (the most important when training neural networks) If you want to do this, You have to create your own one that takes into account the specific issues of this dataset. It is hard and takes time. 

4.1 Sample weights that "stimulate" the network to learn better

4.2 Some fancy incorporation of prior knowledge from the dataset into the network architecture.

About Features 

5.1 If you want a stable solution that also might work well in real life and that will push your skills toward things that are common and you are going to see again and again: try to work on a simple single model (like LGB) and push its score above and above.

You want to try playing around mostly with with lags (df[numeticsl_column] - df.groupby(time_column)[numetical_column].shift(-1)) and agg features (df.groupby(time_column)[numetical_column].agg(std)). these are baseline time-series features. they will work here for sure.

Model Summary

Our model is very simple. To be honest, this result is mostly luck.

model1(private score: -0.196)

Rank return_1day in ascending order

If ExpectedDividend is greater than 0, make it the lowest.

model2:(private score: 0.347 <- this model is 4th place!)

Rank return_1day in descending order.

If ExpectedDividend is greater than 0, make it the lowest.

return_1day is based on the code in this notebook.

return_1day = feats["AdjustedClose"].pct_change(1)

Below is a brief explanation of the reasons for this approach.

Difficulty in model evaluation

https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jpx-tokyo-stock-exchange-prediction/discussion/320323

As noted in the discussion above, the random model scores can be approximated by a normal distribution of μ = 0 and σ = 0.13785. Therefore, even if we were to create a model with a score of -0.3~0.3, we cannot assume that it is a good model. Furthermore, if we create many models, the probability of a good score by chance increases. (Like the multiple comparisons problem in statistics)

Finally, we gave up on creating a ML model and decided to submit a simple approach to avoid errors.

ExpectedDividend

We adopted this variable because we were confident that it would be effective.

The definition of ExpectedDividend column is as follows.

 
Expected dividend value for ex-right date. This value is recorded 2 business days before ex-dividend date.

And, the definition of target column is as follows.

 
Change ratio of adjusted closing price between t+2 and t+1 where t+0 is TradeDate

Thus, since t+2 is the ex-dividend date and t+1 is the date of record, target is likely to be negative.

(However, since ExpectedDividend is almost null, this alone does not get high score…)

Flipped ranking

https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jpx-tokyo-stock-exchange-prediction/discussion/356038

As noted in the discussion above, flipped the ranking and we get a score multiplied by -1. In the competition, we can choose two models, so adopting this strategy will ensure a score > 0.

### 8th Place Solution

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jpx-tokyo-stock-exchange-prediction/discussion/359227

First of all thanks to JPX for hosting such an interesting competition and thanks to all contributors that shared their ideas and code. It helped a lot to understand what the goal was and how to approach it. This was our ( @MrChrissie ´s & my ) first competition in the area of finance and we gained a huge amount of knowledge through other contributions. 

This post is inspired by @flat831 who posted his team solution earlier.

Approach and Model:

After a lot of trial and error, we came up with a relatively simple idea. 

We divided the stocks by the 33SectorName into groups and trained a separate LGBM regressor model for each group. Our entire model then consisted of one LGBM model for each sector. This idea came under the assumption that stocks of the same sector tend to correlate. The prediction target of our model was the change ratio aka. the target column. For the submission, we ranked the predicted change ratio.

Since we ran out of time in the end, a single LGBM model was tuned to all stocks using Optuna and the resulting hyperparameter values were then used for our sub-models. 

We only tuned the following parameters:

num_leaves

max_depth

learning_rate

n_estimators

For the training, we used available features from the stock_price and stock_list files.
