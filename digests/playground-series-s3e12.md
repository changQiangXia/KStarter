Feature Engineering

I also used 10 kfold splits to train models. While the dataset is very small 10 kfolds may seem like too much, but I have tested different values like 3, 5, 7, 8 and 12 and 10 seemed the most optimal for me.

Problem I found with LB scoring

In total, I made 26 submissions for this competition, most of which were aimed at optimizing the model ensemble by trying different combinations of models. Whenever I attempted to change something with feature engineering, the score always fell (not including the outlier part).

As a result, most likely due to my inexperience in competitions, I reached a point where I could no longer achieve a higher score than the one I had already received. Although theoretically, it seemed to me that some solutions should perform better because I took more care to prevent overfitting and ensured that the model performed better on cross-validation. However, I noticed something strange when I started experimenting with the IQR method for outliers and tried different k values. I assumed that most participants are likely to overfit their models for the public leaderboard, even though it only accounts for 20% of all data. Therefore, I decided to choose the most optimal solutions for my final submission. It turned out that my assumptions were still not quite correct.

I had a solution that was similar to the one described above but used fewer models. I excluded KNN and GradientBoosting and used 0.3 and 0.7 quantiles instead of 0.25 and 0.75. This decision resulted in a modest score on the public leaderboard, but would have brought me to 16th place in the current top.

### Two features are enough and contour plots show the truth

If we use only cond and calc in our classifiers, we get better scores than when using all features of the dataset (see below). The nice side effect is that models can easily be compared with the help of contour plots. Look at the six diagrams and judge which classifiers overfit and which ones underfit:

[图 1: inbox%2F7917824%2Fdd4d674ec5653f67f9b4e91f60c2b515%2Fsix-contours.png]

### Why you mustn't use train_test_split

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e12/discussion/401113

When I looked at the 16 most recent public notebooks, wanting to know what cv scores they reported, I was shocked that only three of them cross-validate properly:

[图 1: inbox%2F7917824%2F4b584345ce9dc5cffbc72127909ee223%2Fpie(1).png]

3 notebooks didn't report any score at all. They obviously don't validate their models before submitting.

2 notebooks reported only a training score. The training scores look good, but unfortunately they don't measure generalization performance.

8 notebooks reported a validation score based on train_test_split, usually with a test_size of 0.2. This is wrong.

Only 3 notebooks cross-validated correctly and reported a validation score based on StratifiedKFold.

Validating models with a simple train_test_split is wrong because with such a small validation set the result depends too much on the random seed. Imagine you want to compare LogisticRegression and RandomForest. Depending on the random_state, LogisticRegression looks either 0.057 better than RandomForest or 0.080 worse:

for random_state in [464, 879]:
 X_tr, X_va, y_tr, y_va = train_test_split(train[['cond', 'calc']],
 train[target],
 test_size=0.2,
 random_state=random_state)
 lr = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(random_state=0))
 lr.fit(X_tr, y_tr)
 lr_score = roc_auc_score(y_va, lr.predict_proba(X_va)[:,1])
 rf = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=400, max_depth=2,
 min_samples_leaf=13, max_features=2,
 random_state=0)
 rf.fit(X_tr, y_tr)
 rf_score = roc_auc_score(y_va, rf.predict_proba(X_va)[:,1])
 print(f"random_state={random_state:4}: lr: {lr_score:6.3f} "
 f"rf: {rf_score:6.3f} difference: {lr_score-rf_score:6.3f}")

# random_state= 464: lr: 0.785 rf: 0.864 difference: -0.080
# random_state= 879: lr: 0.822 rf: 0.764 difference: 0.057

We're not talking about the fifth decimal digit here - an error of 0.137 brings you from the top of the leaderboard almost to the bottom!

Now imagine that you used the same train_test_split in the objective function of Optuna: The hyperparameters you get from Optuna would be completely random.

In the current competition, you need at least a StratifiedKFold or better a RepeatedStratifiedKFold to get meaningful results because ten folds are not enough.

### #14th Place Solution

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e12/discussion/402467

As I mentioned in my notebook here, I tried to find the best possible validation score for different feature subsets for 6 models:

Extra Trees

Hist Gradient Boosting

Gradient Boosting

LBGM

Random Forest

XGBoost

For Extra Trees, Random Forest and XGBoost, the feature combination was same. Rest was different. For Hist Gradient Boosting and LGBM, only 3 features combination(though different for both) worked.

Lastly, I used VotingClassifier and added weights as per the scores of all these 6 models. On top of that, I ran the same VotingClassifier for different combination of subsets again and submitted the top 2 CV scores for scoring regardless of what they scored on public leaderboard. I guess I trusted my CV score. But then again, there was a lot of luck.

The CV score of the 2 ensembles submitted were: 

0.8227 with features ['gravity', 'ph', 'cond', 'calc'] 

0.8225 with features ['gravity', 'ph', 'osmo', 'cond', 'calc']

Choosing the best CV score was the best idea as I chose the 2 highest private score submissions. I had some other submissions that were way higher in public leaderboard but not in private leaderboard.

### #24 solution – Xdata + XGB + LGBM + CAT + Opt roc curve

来源：https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/playground-series-s3e12/discussion/402398

Here comes a short summary of the 24th place solution.

Data

Used the competition data + the extra org. data available with no reduction, with only scaling as Feature Engineering.

Models

As it was not an imbalanced target, I used 8 folds training and added LGBM, XGB and CATBoost as models with some before good known parameters for similar problem. Used balanced and weighted settings of the target for the models, to get the target perfect balanced.

After every finished trained model per fold it also did a calibration of the roc curve based on the validation set before predict the probability.

Postprocessing

Run a optimizing of the best weighted value for the three models and also optimizing of the best power average value.
